\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Context}

Project portfolio management (PPM) is a fundamental organizational capability for maintaining competitive advantage and achieving strategic objectives. In project-based industries, portfolio decisions determine which initiatives are selected, prioritized, and funded under limited resources, conflicting criteria, and uncertain execution outcomes \citep{archer1999integrated,cooper2001portfolio,martinsuo2013project,killen2008project}. These decisions become particularly critical in large-scale engineering environments, where organizations must coordinate multiple concurrent projects while balancing strategic value, financial feasibility, schedule pressure, and risk exposure.

In the Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) sector, especially in oil and gas projects, portfolio budgeting is complicated by heterogeneous contract structures, long project durations, capital intensity, and uncertain cash-flow behavior \citep{merrow2011industrial,cui2010systems,kenley1986construction,kaka1993towards}. Budget allocation must account for both cash outflows associated with procurement, labor, equipment, and subcontractors, and delayed cash inflows tied to contractual milestones and client approvals. Consequently, portfolio budgeting is not merely a one-shot allocation problem; it is a sequential and adaptive decision process in which current funding choices influence future project progress, liquidity conditions, and portfolio-level performance.

This challenge is amplified by persistent uncertainty in project execution. Large industrial and infrastructure projects frequently experience delays and cost overruns, and such deviations can propagate through both schedules and financial plans \citep{flyvbjerg2002underestimating,flyvbjerg2003underestimating,love2013probability,love2016relationship}. Earned value indicators such as the Schedule Performance Index (SPI) and Cost Performance Index (CPI) provide a compact representation of execution performance and can support forward-looking control \citep{fleming2016earned,lipke2009prediction,vanhoucke2012integrated}. However, these indicators also highlight the difficulty of making reliable funding decisions before future project states and payment realizations are fully observable. Therefore, portfolio budgeting under cash-flow uncertainty requires methods that can reason across multiple projects, multiple time periods, and uncertain state transitions.

The broader literature on project portfolio management also characterizes these environments by large data volumes, multiple decision criteria, resource constraints, and the need for investment optimization. These characteristics are consistent with prior research on project portfolio selection, project scheduling under uncertainty, and construction cash-flow forecasting \citep{gutjahr2008project,doerner2006pareto,khanzadi2018integrating,golenko1997stochastic,ballesteros2015resource,barraza2007probabilistic}. Taken together, they motivate the use of intelligent decision-support methods when static or manually driven budgeting approaches become insufficient.

\subsection{Research Gap}

A substantial body of literature has addressed project portfolio selection, prioritization, and scheduling through operations research, multi-criteria decision-making, stochastic programming, and metaheuristic optimization \citep{archer1999integrated,liesio2007portfolio,klerides2019stochastic,elshaer2014genetic}. These methods provide valuable structure for complex decision problems, but they are often less suitable when budget allocation must be revised dynamically as new information emerges. In many existing formulations, project cash flows, budget requirements, and performance trajectories are treated as fixed inputs, deterministic plans, or exogenous scenarios. This limits their ability to capture the interaction between funding decisions, execution uncertainty, and future financial states.

A second limitation is that project budgeting in EPC portfolios is inherently sequential. Budget releases made today affect project progress, contractual milestone realization, and future liquidity availability. Yet much of the existing literature focuses on project selection or scheduling as a static or stage-wise optimization exercise rather than a closed-loop control problem. Rolling-horizon approaches partially address this challenge by allowing repeated re-planning over time \citep{chand2002rolling,sahin2013rolling}, but they still depend on the quality of the underlying decision rule and do not by themselves learn adaptive policies from repeated interaction with uncertain environments.

Recent advances in machine learning and reinforcement learning provide a promising direction for this class of problems. Reinforcement learning is specifically designed for sequential decision-making under uncertainty and has demonstrated value in operations, scheduling, and process control settings \citep{puterman1994markov,sutton2018reinforcement,wauters2016learning,chen2018reinforcement,hubbs2020deep,bengio2021machine}. Nevertheless, its application to project portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows remains limited. There is still a need for a reproducible framework that can integrate project-level uncertainty, cash-flow timing, milestone-based payments, and adaptive budget allocation within a portfolio-level decision architecture.

A further practical constraint is data availability. Detailed industrial project portfolio data are typically confidential and difficult to release publicly, which creates a barrier to reproducible experimentation. As a result, the literature lacks sufficiently transparent benchmark environments for testing adaptive portfolio-control methods. This gap motivates the use of literature-calibrated synthetic data as a practical foundation for developing and evaluating learning-based decision-support systems \citep{jordon2022synthetic,xu2019modeling}.

\subsection{Proposed Approach and Contributions}

To address these gaps, this paper develops a deep reinforcement learning framework for optimal project portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows. The problem is modeled as a sequential decision process in which an agent observes the evolving state of a project portfolio and allocates constrained financial resources over time. The state representation incorporates project-level and portfolio-level information, including budget at completion, planned duration, contractual cash-flow patterns, performance indicators, milestone-based payment structures, and liquidity-related variables.

The proposed framework adopts a rolling-horizon control logic so that budget decisions can be revised periodically using newly observed information. This design reflects practical portfolio budgeting behavior in project-based organizations, where financial decisions are revisited as projects advance and uncertainty is gradually resolved. To learn this control policy, the study employs deep reinforcement learning, with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) as the primary training algorithm \citep{schulman2017proximal}, while remaining consistent with the broader reinforcement-learning literature on stochastic control and policy optimization \citep{sutton2018reinforcement,haarnoja2018soft}.

Because real EPC portfolio datasets are typically inaccessible due to confidentiality restrictions, the framework uses a literature-calibrated synthetic data generation process. This allows reproducible experimentation while preserving consistency with known characteristics of project cash flows, schedule variability, and portfolio structure. The synthetic environment is intended as a research testbed that can later be refined and calibrated using anonymized organizational data.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A sequential formulation of project portfolio budgeting:} The study formulates EPC portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows as a dynamic decision problem in which allocations, project progress, and liquidity conditions evolve jointly over time.

    \item \textbf{A custom reinforcement learning environment for portfolio control:} The paper develops an environment that captures project-level uncertainty, cash inflows and outflows, milestone-based payments, and portfolio-level financial constraints.

    \item \textbf{A rolling-horizon adaptive control structure:} The framework introduces a periodic re-planning mechanism that aligns the model with real-world portfolio budgeting practice.

    \item \textbf{A literature-calibrated synthetic data framework:} The study provides a reproducible synthetic portfolio-generation procedure to address the industrial data confidentiality barrier.

    \item \textbf{An AI-based decision-support foundation for portfolio management:} The resulting framework supports adaptive allocation policies, scenario simulation, and future deployment with organization-specific data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Paper Organization}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formulates the project portfolio budgeting problem under uncertain cash flows.
