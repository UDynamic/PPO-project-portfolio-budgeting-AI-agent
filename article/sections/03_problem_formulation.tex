
\subsection{introduction}
This formulation presents a general stochastic resource allocation problem for managing contracted project portfolios under financial constraints and operational uncertainty. The problem structure is applicable across construction \cite{kenley1986relationship}, software development, defense contracting \cite{christensen1993empirical}, and other project-intensive sectors as The proposed model is created integrating project management domain knowledge and methodologies such as S-curve expenditure dynamics \cite{kenley1986relationship}, Earned Value Management (EVM) performance tracking \cite{fleming2016earned}, milestone-based payment structures, performance recovery intended interventions, and abandonment options \cite{dixit1994investment} and project Termination.

The formulation extends earned-value management frameworks by embedding them within a constrained Markov decision process \cite{altman1999constrained} with stochastic state transitions and multi-objective performance evaluation, while remaining solution-agnostic and compatible with diverse solution methodologies including reinforcement learning, approximate dynamic programming, or scenario-based stochastic optimization.

\subsection{scope}
The focus is to go one step beyond traditional project selection models into the less studied realm of operational control of already selected projects, taking sequential budget allocation decisions across portfolio to maximize utility of the stakeholders.  

\textbf{the proposed model is a generalized model. each specific design is introduced with the most studied and known quality in the literature. this model is configurable to any specific industry or sector in the scope defined.}

\subsection{fundamental designs}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A portfolio is a collection of projects.
    \item Each project is described by the contractual attributes known before commitment.
    \item there are two parties in each project: contractor and client. the contractor delivers work and the client pays for the service or product.
    \item The resource allocation problem is modeled only by the budget allocation across project portfolio
    \item Temporal dimension is considered discrete timesteps across the portfolio, representing calendar months because the key strategic meetings are conventionally held monthly.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Planned duration:} Let \(s_i\) and \(f_i\) denote the planned start and planned finish periods, respectively. The planned duration is
\[
D_i^{\mathrm{plan}} = f_i-s_i+1,
\]
and the contractual planned execution window is \(\mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}}=\{t:s_i\leq t\leq f_i\}\). (this is specifically useful when the actual progress deviates the plan).

\item \textbf{planned execution window:}

\item \textbf{Budget at completion:} The budget at completion as the total sum of expenditure from contractor \(\mathrm{BAC}_i\) represents the planned cost basis of the project.

\item \textbf{Contract price and profit margin:} The contract price as the total sum of payment from client is \(CP_i=\mathrm{BAC}_i(1+\pi_i)\), where \(\pi_i\) is the contractual profit margin.

\item \textbf{Advance payment ratio:} A contractually fixed percentage of \(\mathrm{BAC}_i\) which is advance payment ratio \(\alpha_i\in[0,1]\).

\item \textbf{Retention ratio:} A contractually fixed percentage withheld from each payment which is retention ratio \(\rho_i\in[0,1]\).

\item \textbf{Milestone structure:} A finite set of milestones \(\mathcal{M}_i=\{1,2,\ldots,M_i\}\) to be delivered by the contractor, where each milestone \(j\in\mathcal{M}_i\) is defined by a progress threshold \(\theta_{i,j}\in(0,1]\) and a payment weight \(\phi_{i,j}>0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i}\phi_{i,j}=1\).

\item \textbf{Delay penalty parameters:} A delay penalty to enforce on time completion of the project as delay penalty rate \(d_i\geq 0\) (penalty per unit delay per period) and a delay penalty cap \(D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}\geq 0\) (maximum cumulative delay penalty as a fraction of contract price).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Planned Expenditure Profile}
\label{subsubsec:planned_expenditure}

The planned cumulative expenditure of project \(i\) follows a normalized S-curve \cite{kenley1986relationship,barraza2004probabilistic}. The normalized planned time is \(\tau_{i,t}=(t-s_i+1)/D_i^{\mathrm{plan}}\) for \(t\in \mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}}\), with truncation \(\tau_{i,t}^{c}=\min\{1,\max\{0,\tau_{i,t}\}\}\).

The planned cumulative expenditure fraction is modeled as \(F_i(\tau)=I_{\tau}(a_i,b_i)\), where \(I_{\tau}(\cdot,\cdot)\) is the regularized incomplete Beta function and \(a_i,b_i>0\) are shape parameters. The planned cumulative expenditure is \(\bar{E}_{i,t}=\mathrm{BAC}_i F_i(\tau_{i,t}^{c})\), and the planned period expenditure is \(\Delta\bar{E}_{i,t}=\bar{E}_{i,t}-\bar{E}_{i,t-1}\).

The planned cumulative progress is assumed to track planned expenditure: \(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}=F_i(\tau_{i,t}^{c})\).

\subsubsection{State Variables}
\label{subsubsec:project_state}

At each period \(t\), project \(i\) is characterized by the following state variables:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Actual cumulative progress:} \(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\in[0,1]\), the fraction of project scope completed by period \(t\).

\item \textbf{Cumulative actual expenditure:} \(E_{i,t}\geq 0\), the total expenditure incurred on project \(i\) up to period \(t\).

\item \textbf{Schedule Performance Index (SPI):} 
\[
\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}=\frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}}{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}}.
\]

\item \textbf{Cost Performance Index (CPI):}
\[
\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}=\frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\cdot\mathrm{BAC}_i}{E_{i,t}}.
\]

\item \textbf{Remaining duration estimate:} \(RD_{i,t}\), the estimated number of periods required to complete the remaining work, computed using EVM-based forecasting methods \cite{lipke2008prediction}.

\item \textbf{Project status:} A categorical variable indicating whether the project is active, completed, or terminated.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Cash-Flow Mechanics}
\label{subsubsec:project_cashflow}

Project cash flows consist of outflows (expenditures) and inflows (contractual payments). The payment structure includes advance payment, interim milestone payments, and final payment with retention release.

\paragraph{Advance Payment and Recovery Mechanism}

In EPC and EPC/Turnkey projects, advance payment constitutes a contractual mechanism through which a portion of the Contract Price is disbursed to the contractor at an early stage of the project, prior to the certification of corresponding physical progress. Industry-standard contract forms and empirical studies consistently characterise advance payment as an early liquidity support instrument, secured by an Advance Payment Guarantee and contractually recovered through subsequent milestone payments \cite{fidic2017silver,cidb2015construction,eggers2019lump}.

\textbf{Advance Payment Disbursement:}
At project initiation (period \(s_i\)), the contractor receives an advance payment of amount:
\[
A_i=\alpha_i\cdot CP_i,
\]
where \(\alpha_i\) is the contractually fixed advance payment ratio and \(CP_i\) is the Contract Price.

\textbf{Recovery Schedule:}
The advance payment is recovered through proportional deductions from certified interim milestone payments. Let \(R_{i,j}\) denote the gross milestone payment corresponding to milestone \(j\in\mathcal{M}_i\). The recovery deduction applied to milestone \(j\) is:
\[
\Delta A_{i,j}=\phi_{i,j}\cdot A_i,
\]
where \(\phi_{i,j}\) is the milestone payment weight. The net milestone payment received by the contractor is:
\[
R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}=R_{i,j}-\Delta A_{i,j}.
\]

\textbf{Total Milestone Payments:}
The sum of all gross milestone payments (interim and final) equals 100\% of the Contract Price:
\[
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i}R_{i,j}=CP_i.
\]
After full recovery, the cumulative net payments received equal the Contract Price:
\[
A_i+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i}R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}=CP_i.
\]

\textbf{Retention and Independence:}
Retention is applied to milestone payments as a separate mechanism:
\[
R_{i,j}=\phi_{i,j}\cdot CP_i\cdot(1-\rho_i),
\]
where \(\rho_i\) is the retention ratio. Advance payment recovery and retention are independent: recovery deductions are computed on gross payments before retention, and retained amounts are released at project completion. The two mechanisms do not interact.

\textbf{No Debt Characterisation:}
Advance payment is not characterised as debt in the contractor's financial accounts. It carries no interest obligation and creates no balance-sheet liability. It is a contractual timing adjustment of payment flows, not a financing instrument. This treatment is consistent with industry accounting standards and prevents double-counting of financing costs.

\paragraph{Milestone-Based Revenue Recognition}

Milestone \(j\in\mathcal{M}_i\) is certified and its corresponding payment is released when actual progress reaches the milestone threshold:
\[
P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\geq\theta_{i,j}.
\]
The gross milestone revenue is \(R_{i,j}=\phi_{i,j}\cdot CP_i\cdot(1-\rho_i)\), and the net payment after advance recovery is \(R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}=R_{i,j}-\Delta A_{i,j}\).

\paragraph{Final Payment and Retention Release}

Upon project completion (\(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}=1\)), all milestone payments including the final milestone are certified, and the retained amount is released:
\[
R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}=\rho_i\cdot CP_i.
\]

\paragraph{Delay Penalty}

Contractual delay penalties are applied based on the deviation between actual and planned completion dates. Let \(t_i^{\mathrm{actual}}\) denote the actual completion period and \(f_i\) the planned finish period. The delay is:
\[
\Delta t_i=\max\{0,t_i^{\mathrm{actual}}-f_i\}.
\]

According to industry practice \cite{bryde2006project,flyvbjerg2003underestimating}, client-caused delays (e.g., payment delays, scope changes) are deducted from the total delay, and the contractor is conventionally responsible for up to 50\% of the remaining delay. Let \(\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{client}}\) denote client-caused delay and \(\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{contractor}}\) denote contractor-responsible delay:
\[
\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{contractor}}=\min\left\{0.5\cdot(\Delta t_i-\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{client}}),\frac{D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}}{d_i}\right\},
\]
where \(d_i\) is the delay penalty rate per period and \(D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}\) is the delay penalty cap as a fraction of contract price.

The delay penalty deducted from the final payment is:
\[
DP_i=d_i\cdot\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{contractor}}\cdot CP_i.
\]

The net final payment is:
\[
R_i^{\mathrm{final}}=R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}+R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}-DP_i,
\]
where \(R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}\) is the net final milestone payment.

\subsubsection{Budget Allocation and Stochastic Performance Response}
\label{subsubsec:allocation_response}

At each period \(t\), the decision-maker allocates a budget amount \(x_{i,t}\geq 0\) to project \(i\). The allocation does not deterministically translate into progress. Instead, the realized progress increment \(\Delta P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\) is stochastic and depends on the allocation amount, the project's current state, and an uncertain efficiency parameter \(\eta_{i,t}\).

The budget-to-progress conversion is modeled as:
\[
\Delta P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}=\eta_{i,t}\cdot\frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
\]
where \(\eta_{i,t}\) is a stochastic efficiency parameter representing the contractor's realized productivity in period \(t\). The efficiency parameter captures all sources of uncertainty in the contractor's response to budget allocation, including workforce productivity, supply chain disruptions, weather conditions, technical difficulties, and coordination issues. The distribution of \(\eta_{i,t}\) is not specified in the problem formulation and is calibrated empirically in the Methodology section using historical project performance data.

The actual cumulative progress and expenditure evolve as:
\[
P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}=P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}+\Delta P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}},
\]
\[
E_{i,t}=E_{i,t-1}+x_{i,t}.
\]

The EVM indices (SPI and CPI) are updated based on the new progress and expenditure values.

\subsubsection{Performance-Triggered Intervention}
\label{subsubsec:intervention}

When a project exhibits persistent underperformance, the environment (external to the decision-maker, representing senior management or the client) may trigger a corrective intervention. The intervention is modeled as a single performance-recovery mechanism: after observing sustained schedule delay beyond a project-specific tolerance threshold, the environment initiates a one-time corrective action that incurs a fixed cost and attempts to restore schedule performance through managerial attention, resource reallocation, or process adjustments.

Let \(\Delta_{i,t}=P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}-P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}\) denote the schedule delay (progress shortfall) at period \(t\). The intervention is triggered when:
\[
\Delta_{i,t}>\tau_i,
\]
where \(\tau_i\) is a project-specific delay tolerance threshold.

When triggered, the environment incurs an intervention cost \(\chi_i\) and mandates a minimum allocation to the project in the subsequent period:
\[
m_{i,t}=m_{i,t}^{0}+\chi_i\cdot I_{i,t-1},
\]
where \(m_{i,t}^{0}\) is the baseline mandatory allocation, \(I_{i,t-1}\in\{0,1\}\) is the intervention indicator, and \(\chi_i\) is the intervention cost.

The intervention is a single-attempt mechanism. If performance does not recover after the intervention (i.e., delay persists above threshold despite the mandated allocation), no further intervention is triggered, and the project proceeds toward potential termination. This design reflects the empirical observation that repeated interventions on persistently underperforming projects yield diminishing returns \cite{merrow2011industrial}.

\subsubsection{Termination Conditions and Settlement}
\label{subsubsec:termination}

A project may be terminated before completion if three conditions are simultaneously satisfied:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sustained schedule delay exceeding penalty horizon:} The expected remaining delay at completion exceeds the delay penalty cap horizon:
\[
\mathbb{E}[\Delta t_i]>\frac{D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}}{d_i}.
\]
This indicates that the project is expected to incur the maximum contractual delay penalty, at which point the client loses leverage over the contractor.

\item \textbf{Post-intervention non-recovery:} An intervention has been triggered at some prior period \(t'<t\), but performance has not recovered (delay remains above \(\tau_i\) for multiple periods following the intervention). This signals that the intervention has failed to restore performance, and further corrective action is unlikely to succeed.

\item \textbf{Non-allocation precedent:} The decision-maker has allocated zero or minimal budget to the project for a sustained period (e.g., \(x_{i,t}=m_{i,t}^{0}\) for multiple consecutive periods), signaling abandonment intent and indicating that continued execution is economically unattractive.
\end{enumerate}

When all three conditions are met, the environment forces project termination and initiates the settlement mechanism. The simultaneous satisfaction of all three conditions ensures that termination occurs only when the project is both contractually disadvantageous (condition 1) and operationally non-viable (conditions 2 and 3).

\paragraph{Termination Settlement Mechanism}

At termination (period \(t_i^{\mathrm{term}}\)), the project is incomplete (\(P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}}<1\)). The settlement involves a final financial balancing based on completed work, advance payment recovery status, and contractual obligations.

The contractor is entitled to payment for certified progress completed up to termination. The gross value of completed work is:
\[
V_i^{\mathrm{term}}=P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}}\cdot CP_i.
\]

The net settlement payment accounts for retention and advance payment recovery. Let \(\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}\) denote the set of milestones certified up to termination:
\[
\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}=\{j\in\mathcal{M}_i:\theta_{i,j}\leq P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}}\}.
\]

The cumulative advance payment recovery deductions already applied through certified milestones are:
\[
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}}\Delta A_{i,j}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}}\phi_{i,j}\cdot A_i.
\]

The outstanding advance payment balance is:
\[
A_i^{\mathrm{outstanding}}=A_i-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}}\Delta A_{i,j}.
\]

The net settlement payment is:
\[
R_i^{\mathrm{term}}=V_i^{\mathrm{term}}\cdot(1-\rho_i)-A_i^{\mathrm{outstanding}}.
\]

No delay penalty is applied at termination, as the project has not reached contractual completion and the delay penalty mechanism is tied to completion date. However, if \(R_i^{\mathrm{term}}<0\), the settlement results in a net financial obligation for the contractor (advance payment not fully recovered through completed work). In this case, the environment enforces a mandatory allocation from available portfolio cash to settle this obligation using a priority queue mechanism: available cash inflows from other projects are directed to the terminated project's settlement before other portfolio commitments, until the settlement balance is cleared or available cash is exhausted.

This settlement mechanism is consistent with standard EPC contract termination clauses \cite{fidic2017silver} and ensures that advance payment recovery obligations are honored even under premature termination.

\subsection{Portfolio Aggregation and Decision Problem}
\label{subsec:portfolio_aggregation}

The portfolio \(\mathcal{P}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\) is the aggregation of \(n\) projects, each following the dynamics defined in Section \ref{subsec:single_project}. The planning horizon is indexed by discrete periods \(t\in\mathcal{T}=\{1,\ldots,H\}\).

\subsubsection{Portfolio State}

At each period \(t\), the portfolio state \(S_t\) consists of the individual project states and the aggregate financial position:
\[
S_t=\left\{\{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}},E_{i,t},\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t},RD_{i,t},\text{status}_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{P}},B_t\right\},
\]
where \(B_t\) is the available budget (cash balance) at the beginning of period \(t\).

The set of active projects at period \(t\) is:
\[
\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}=\{i\in\mathcal{P}:\text{status}_i=\text{active},\,s_i\leq t\leq f_i+\text{buffer}_i\},
\]
where \(\text{buffer}_i\) is an allowable delay buffer beyond the planned finish date.

\subsubsection{Portfolio-Level Budget Constraint}

The decision-maker faces a hard portfolio-level budget constraint. The total allocation across all active projects must satisfy:
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}\leq B_t,
\]
where \(B_t\) is the available budget at period \(t\).

The budget evolves according to the cash-flow dynamics:
\[
B_{t+1}=B_t-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}+V_t-C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}},
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}\) is the total operational expenditure allocated to active projects,
\item \(V_t\) is the total milestone-based revenue received in period \(t\),
\item \(C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}}\) is the total intervention cost incurred in period \(t\).
\end{itemize}

The hard budget constraint reflects the elimination of the endogenous credit system. Organizations must manage portfolio execution within available cash, eliminating the soft budget constraint and associated credit dynamics from the formulation.

\subsubsection{Objective Function}

The decision-maker seeks to maximize the discounted cumulative portfolio performance:
\[
\max_{\mu}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\gamma^{t-1}R_t+\gamma^{H}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}R_i^{\mathrm{final}}\right],
\]
where \(\mu\) is a policy mapping states to allocation decisions, \(\gamma\in(0,1]\) is a discount factor, and \(R_t\) is the period performance metric.

The period performance is defined as:
\[
R_t=V_t-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}-C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}}-C_t^{\mathrm{delay}},
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(V_t\) is the total milestone-based revenue received in period \(t\):
\[
V_t=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i}R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\text{milestone }(i,j)\text{ is newly certified at }t\}.
\]

\item \(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}\) is the total operational expenditure.

\item \(C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\chi_i\cdot I_{i,t}\) is the total intervention cost incurred in period \(t\).

\item \(C_t^{\mathrm{delay}}\) is the imputed delay-related cost, representing the opportunity cost, reputational impact, or resource idleness associated with schedule delays across the portfolio. This may be modeled as a weighted sum of project-level delay indicators or a portfolio-level delay penalty function.
\end{itemize}

At the terminal period \(H\), final payments from completed projects are released:
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}R_i^{\mathrm{final}}=\sum_{i:\text{status}_i=\text{completed}}(R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}+R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}-DP_i),
\]
where \(R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}\) is the net final milestone payment, \(R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}\) is the retention release, and \(DP_i\) is the delay penalty.

\subsubsection{Decision Policy and Constraints}

The allocation decision at period \(t\) is a vector \(x_t=\{x_{i,t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}\), where each \(x_{i,t}\) must satisfy:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mandatory minimum allocation:} 
\[
x_{i,t}\geq m_{i,t}=m_{i,t}^{0}+\chi_i\cdot I_{i,t-1},
\]
where \(m_{i,t}^{0}\) is the baseline mandatory minimum and \(I_{i,t-1}\) is the intervention indicator from the previous period.

\item \textbf{Portfolio budget constraint:}
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}\leq B_t.
\]

\item \textbf{Non-negativity:}
\[
x_{i,t}\geq 0.
\]
\end{enumerate}

The policy \(\mu:S_t\to x_t\) is solution-agnostic and may be implemented using reinforcement learning (e.g., Proximal Policy Optimization), approximate dynamic programming, or scenario-based stochastic optimization methods. The formulation imposes no restrictions on the solution methodology.

\subsection{Summary of Problem Structure}

The problem is a finite-horizon, stochastic, constrained sequential decision problem under uncertainty. The key structural features are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{State space:} High-dimensional and continuous, consisting of project-level progress, expenditure, performance indices, remaining duration estimates, and aggregate cash balance. The state dimension scales linearly with the number of projects.

\item \textbf{Action space:} Continuous allocation decisions subject to project-specific mandatory minimum constraints and a portfolio-level budget constraint.

\item \textbf{Stochasticity:} Budget-to-progress efficiency is stochastic and realized only after allocation. Intervention triggers, termination conditions, and client payment timing introduce additional stochastic elements.

\item \textbf{Constraints:} Mandatory minimum allocations (baseline and intervention-triggered), hard budget constraints, advance payment recovery schedules, and termination settlement obligations.

\item \textbf{Objective:} Multi-period cumulative discounted performance integrating milestone revenues, operational expenditures, intervention costs, delay penalties, and final payments (including retention releases and delay penalty deductions).

\item \textbf{Sequential structure:} Decisions at period \(t\) affect the state at period \(t+1\) through stochastic project performance responses, milestone certifications, and budget evolution. The problem exhibits the Markov property: the future state depends only on the current state and current action, not on the full history.

\item \textbf{Solution methodology:} The formulation is compatible with model-free reinforcement learning (when the transition dynamics are unknown or too complex to model explicitly), model-based dynamic programming (when transition probabilities can be estimated from data), or hybrid approaches combining learned policies with domain knowledge. The solution method is specified in the Methodology section.
\end{itemize}

This problem structure generalizes classical project portfolio budget allocation by integrating contractual cash-flow mechanics (advance payment, milestone payments, retention, delay penalties), stochastic performance dynamics (budget-to-progress efficiency uncertainty), performance-triggered interventions, termination conditions with settlement mechanisms, and multi-objective performance evaluation within a unified sequential decision framework applicable across construction, software, defense, and other project-intensive sectors.

The formulation is distribution-free and parametric: all stochastic elements (efficiency parameter distributions, intervention trigger probabilities, termination condition thresholds) are treated as parameters to be calibrated from empirical data in the Methodology section. This separation preserves the generality of the problem structure while allowing sector-specific instantiation through data-driven calibration.

\subsection{Operations Research Formulation}
\label{subsec:or_formulation}

The sequential decision problem described in Sections~\ref{subsec:single_project}--\ref{subsec:portfolio_aggregation} admits a direct restatement as a constrained mathematical program. This restatement is solution-method-agnostic: it specifies sets, parameters, decision variables, an objective function, and constraints, without reference to states, actions, policies, or value functions. The program below is solvable, in principle, by stochastic programming, scenario-based optimization, approximate dynamic programming, or any other classical operations-research method; reinforcement learning, discussed elsewhere in this paper, is one possible solution approach among several and is not assumed in what follows. Every constraint and dynamic relation stated here is a direct translation of an equation already given in Sections~\ref{subsec:single_project}--\ref{subsec:portfolio_aggregation}; no new mechanisms are introduced.

Several elements of the original formulation are stochastic or depend on forward-looking (expectation-based) quantities. Where a constraint cannot be written as a deterministic inequality without altering its meaning, this is flagged explicitly in Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_uncertainty_handling}, together with the standard OR treatment (scenario-based recourse, expectation operators, or chance constraints) rather than a silent deterministic substitute.

\subsubsection{Sets and Indices}
\label{subsubsec:or_sets}

\begin{description}
\item[\(\mathcal{P}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\)] set of projects in the portfolio, indexed by \(i\).
\item[\(\mathcal{T}=\{1,\ldots,H\}\)] set of discrete planning periods, indexed by \(t\).
\item[\(\mathcal{M}_i=\{1,\ldots,M_i\}\)] set of contractual milestones for project \(i\), indexed by \(j\).
\item[\(\mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}}=\{t:s_i\leq t\leq f_i\}\)] planned execution window of project \(i\).
\item[\(\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}\subseteq\mathcal{P}\)] subset of projects active at period \(t\) (defined endogenously by the status recursion in Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_state_recursions}).
\item[\(\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_i\)] subset of milestones of project \(i\) certified up to the period under consideration.
\item[\(\Omega\)] sample space of realizations of the stochastic primitives (efficiency parameters, client-caused delay, intervention triggers); used only in the scenario-based restatement of Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_uncertainty_handling}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Parameters}
\label{subsubsec:or_parameters}

All parameters below are contractually fixed or empirically calibrated, as defined in Section~\ref{subsec:single_project}; none are introduced here.

\begin{description}
\item[\(s_i,f_i\)] planned start and finish periods of project \(i\); \(D_i^{\mathrm{plan}}=f_i-s_i+1\).
\item[\(\mathrm{BAC}_i\)] budget at completion of project \(i\).
\item[\(\pi_i\)] contractual profit margin; \(CP_i=\mathrm{BAC}_i(1+\pi_i)\).
\item[\(\alpha_i\)] advance payment ratio, \(\alpha_i\in[0,1]\); \(A_i=\alpha_i CP_i\).
\item[\(\rho_i\)] retention ratio, \(\rho_i\in[0,1]\).
\item[\(\theta_{i,j},\phi_{i,j}\)] progress threshold and payment weight of milestone \(j\) of project \(i\), with \(\theta_{i,j}\in(0,1]\) and \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i}\phi_{i,j}=1\).
\item[\(d_i,D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}\)] delay penalty rate per period and delay penalty cap (as a fraction of contract price) for project \(i\).
\item[\(a_i,b_i\)] shape parameters of the planned S-curve \(F_i(\tau)=I_\tau(a_i,b_i)\).
\item[\(\tau_i\)] project-specific schedule delay tolerance threshold triggering intervention.
\item[\(\chi_i\)] fixed cost of a corrective intervention on project \(i\).
\item[\(m_{i,t}^{0}\)] baseline mandatory minimum allocation to project \(i\) at period \(t\).
\item[\(\mathrm{buffer}_i\)] allowable delay buffer beyond the planned finish date, used in the definition of \(\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}\).
\item[{\(\gamma\in(0,1]\)}] discount factor.
\item[\(B_1\)] initial portfolio cash balance.
\item[\(\eta_{i,t}\)] stochastic budget-to-progress efficiency parameter (random primitive; not a decision parameter).
\item[\(\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{client}}\)] client-caused delay attributed to project \(i\) (exogenous, contractually determined).
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Decision Variables}
\label{subsubsec:or_decision_variables}

\begin{description}
\item[\(x_{i,t}\geq 0\)] budget allocated to project \(i\) at period \(t\), for \(i\in\mathcal{P}\), \(t\in\mathcal{T}\). This is the only class of decision variable in the program; all other quantities below are state-dependent variables determined by \(\{x_{i,t}\}\) through the recursions of Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_state_recursions}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Auxiliary (State-Dependent) Variables}
\label{subsubsec:or_auxiliary_variables}

The following quantities are not independently chosen; each is a deterministic or stochastic function of the allocation decisions \(\{x_{i,t}\}\) and the realized efficiency parameters \(\{\eta_{i,t}\}\), as defined by the recursions in Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_state_recursions}. They are listed here because they appear in the objective and constraints below.

\begin{description}
\item[{\(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\in[0,1]\)}] cumulative actual progress of project \(i\) at period \(t\).
\item[\(E_{i,t}\geq 0\)] cumulative actual expenditure on project \(i\) up to period \(t\).
\item[\(\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}\)] schedule and cost performance indices of project \(i\) at period \(t\).
\item[\(B_t\geq 0\)] portfolio cash balance at the beginning of period \(t\).
\item[\(I_{i,t}\in\{0,1\}\)] intervention indicator for project \(i\) at period \(t\) (\(1\) if a corrective intervention is triggered, else \(0\)).
\item[\(\mathrm{status}_{i,t}\in\{\text{active},\text{completed},\text{terminated}\}\)] categorical status of project \(i\) at period \(t\).
\item[\(R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}\)] net payment for milestone \(j\) of project \(i\), released in the period in which \(j\) is newly certified.
\item[\(R_i^{\mathrm{final}}\)] net final payment for project \(i\) (released on completion).
\item[\(R_i^{\mathrm{term}}\)] net settlement payment for project \(i\) (released on termination, possibly negative).
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Objective Function}
\label{subsubsec:or_objective}

The decision-maker chooses the allocation schedule \(\{x_{i,t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{P},t\in\mathcal{T}}\) to maximize expected discounted net portfolio cash flow:
\[
\max_{\{x_{i,t}\}}\ \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\gamma^{t-1}\left(V_t-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}-C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}}-C_t^{\mathrm{delay}}\right)+\gamma^{H}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}R_i^{\mathrm{final}}\right],
\]
where the expectation is taken over the joint distribution of the stochastic primitives \(\{\eta_{i,t}\}\) and any other random elements entering the state-dependent variables (Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_uncertainty_handling}), and:
\[
V_t=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i}R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\text{milestone }(i,j)\text{ is newly certified at }t\},\qquad
C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\chi_i I_{i,t},
\]
with \(C_t^{\mathrm{delay}}\) the portfolio-level imputed delay cost as defined following the objective in the original formulation. This objective is identical in content to the discounted cumulative performance criterion stated in the original formulation; it is restated here purely as the objective of a mathematical program, with no policy or value-function interpretation implied.

\subsubsection{Decision Constraints}
\label{subsubsec:or_decision_constraints}

The allocation variables are subject to three constraints, each carried over directly from the original formulation.

\paragraph{Non-negativity.}
\[
x_{i,t}\geq 0,\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P},\ t\in\mathcal{T}.
\tag{C1}
\]

\paragraph{Mandatory minimum allocation.}
\[
x_{i,t}\geq m_{i,t}=m_{i,t}^{0}+\chi_i\cdot I_{i,t-1},\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}},\ t\in\mathcal{T}.
\tag{C2}
\]

\paragraph{Portfolio budget constraint.}
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}\leq B_t,\qquad\forall t\in\mathcal{T}.
\tag{C3}
\]
This is a hard constraint: no borrowing or credit mechanism is available, consistent with the elimination of the soft budget constraint in the original formulation.

\subsubsection{State Recursion Constraints}
\label{subsubsec:or_state_recursions}

The auxiliary variables of Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_auxiliary_variables} are linked across periods by the following equality and inequality constraints, each a direct restatement of the corresponding dynamic equation in Section~\ref{subsec:single_project}.

\paragraph{Progress and expenditure recursion.}
\[
P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}=P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}+\eta_{i,t}\cdot\frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},\qquad
E_{i,t}=E_{i,t-1}+x_{i,t},\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P},\ t\in\mathcal{T},
\tag{C4}
\]
with \(P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}}=0\), \(E_{i,0}=0\). The efficiency parameter \(\eta_{i,t}\) is a random primitive realized after \(x_{i,t}\) is chosen; see Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_uncertainty_handling}.

\paragraph{Planned progress and EVM indices.}
\[
P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}=F_i(\tau_{i,t}^{c}),\qquad
\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}=\frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}}{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}},\qquad
\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}=\frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\cdot\mathrm{BAC}_i}{E_{i,t}},\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P},\ t\in\mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}},
\tag{C5}
\]
with \(\tau_{i,t}^c\), \(F_i(\cdot)\) as defined in Section~\ref{subsubsec:planned_expenditure}. These are deterministic algebraic definitions, not independent constraints, but are listed here as they enter the termination conditions (C9)--(C11) and the active-set definition (C13).

\paragraph{Portfolio cash balance recursion.}
\[
B_{t+1}=B_t-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}x_{i,t}+V_t-C_t^{\mathrm{intervention}},\qquad\forall t\in\mathcal{T},
\tag{C6}
\]
with \(B_1\) given. This is the same recursion as in the original formulation; \(B_t\geq 0\) is enforced jointly by (C3) and (C6).

\subsubsection{Payment Mechanics Constraints}
\label{subsubsec:or_payment_constraints}

\paragraph{Milestone certification and net payment.}
Milestone \(j\) of project \(i\) is certified at the first period \(t\) such that \(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\geq\theta_{i,j}\). On certification:
\[
R_{i,j}=\phi_{i,j}\cdot CP_i\cdot(1-\rho_i),\qquad
\Delta A_{i,j}=\phi_{i,j}\cdot A_i,\qquad
R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}=R_{i,j}-\Delta A_{i,j},\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P},\ j\in\mathcal{M}_i.
\tag{C7}
\]

\paragraph{Final payment and retention release.}
On completion (\(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}=1\)):
\[
R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}=\rho_i\cdot CP_i,\qquad
R_i^{\mathrm{final}}=R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}+R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}-DP_i,\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P},
\tag{C8}
\]
with the delay penalty \(DP_i=d_i\cdot\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{contractor}}\cdot CP_i\) and \(\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{contractor}}\) defined exactly as in Section~\ref{subsubsec:project_cashflow}, including the 50\% contractor-responsibility convention and the delay penalty cap.

\subsubsection{Intervention and Termination Constraints}
\label{subsubsec:or_intervention_termination}

\paragraph{Intervention trigger.}
Let \(\Delta_{i,t}=P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}-P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}\). The intervention indicator is determined by:
\[
I_{i,t}=\mathbf{1}\{\Delta_{i,t}>\tau_i\},\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal{P},\ t\in\mathcal{T}.
\tag{C9}
\]
This is a deterministic indicator constraint given \(P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}\) and \(P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}\); it feeds the mandatory minimum allocation (C2) in the following period.

\paragraph{Termination conditions.}
Project \(i\) is forced to terminate at the first period \(t\) at which all three of the following hold simultaneously, exactly as in the original formulation:
\[
\text{(i)}\ \ \mathbb{E}[\Delta t_i \mid \mathcal{F}_t]>\frac{D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}}{d_i},\qquad
\text{(ii)}\ \ \exists\, t'<t:\ I_{i,t'}=1 \text{ and } \Delta_{i,\tilde t}>\tau_i\ \forall \tilde t\in(t',t],
\tag{C10}
\]
\[
\text{(iii)}\ \ x_{i,\tilde t}=m_{i,\tilde t}^{0}\ \ \forall\,\tilde t\in\{t-k+1,\ldots,t\}\text{ for a specified run length }k.
\tag{C11}
\]
Condition (i) is a forward-looking, expectation-based condition: \(\mathbb{E}[\Delta t_i\mid\mathcal{F}_t]\) is the expected total delay at completion given information available at \(t\) (filtration \(\mathcal{F}_t\)), and is \emph{not} a deterministic function of decisions up to \(t\) alone — see the flag in Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_uncertainty_handling}. Conditions (ii) and (iii) are deterministic given the realized history of \(I_{i,\cdot}\), \(\Delta_{i,\cdot}\), and \(x_{i,\cdot}\), and are stated here as logical (state-transition) constraints governing the evolution of \(\mathrm{status}_{i,t}\), not as constraints on the decision-maker's choice of \(x_{i,t}\):
\[
\mathrm{status}_{i,t}=\text{terminated}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{(i)}\wedge\text{(ii)}\wedge\text{(iii) all hold at }t.
\tag{C12}
\]

\paragraph{Termination settlement.}
If \(\mathrm{status}_{i,t}\) transitions to terminated at \(t_i^{\mathrm{term}}\):
\[
V_i^{\mathrm{term}}=P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}}\cdot CP_i,\qquad
A_i^{\mathrm{outstanding}}=A_i-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}}\Delta A_{i,j},\qquad
R_i^{\mathrm{term}}=V_i^{\mathrm{term}}(1-\rho_i)-A_i^{\mathrm{outstanding}}.
\tag{C13}
\]
No delay penalty applies at termination, as in the original formulation. If \(R_i^{\mathrm{term}}<0\), the outstanding obligation is cleared from available portfolio cash inflows ahead of other commitments, via the same priority-queue mechanism described in Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination}; this is a settlement-ordering rule on the cash recursion (C6), not an additional decision variable.

\paragraph{Active project set.}
\[
\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}=\{i\in\mathcal{P}:\mathrm{status}_{i,t}=\text{active},\ s_i\leq t\leq f_i+\mathrm{buffer}_i\},\qquad\forall t\in\mathcal{T}.
\tag{C14}
\]
This set is endogenous: it depends on the realized status trajectory determined by (C9)--(C12), and in turn determines which projects are subject to (C2) and (C3) at \(t\).

\subsubsection{Treatment of Stochastic and Forward-Looking Elements}
\label{subsubsec:or_uncertainty_handling}

The program (C1)--(C14) as written mixes deterministic algebraic constraints with three elements that cannot be reduced to deterministic inequalities without changing their meaning. Each is flagged here individually, with the standard OR treatment proposed rather than a silent deterministic substitution. The choice among these treatments is a methodological decision for the Methodology section and is not resolved here.

\paragraph{(a) Budget-to-progress efficiency \(\eta_{i,t}\).}
The progress recursion (C4) contains a random primitive realized only after \(x_{i,t}\) is chosen. This is the defining feature of a recourse problem: \(x_{i,t}\) is a here-and-now decision, while the resulting progress and downstream consequences (milestone certification, intervention triggers, termination) are wait-and-see quantities. The standard OR treatment is a multi-stage stochastic program with recourse, in which \(\eta_{i,t}\) is represented either by a finite scenario tree (scenario-based stochastic programming) or, where a closed-form distribution is calibrated, by the expectation operator already present in the objective of Section~\ref{subsubsec:or_objective} \cite{birge2011introduction}. Under a scenario-tree representation, the decision variables \(x_{i,t}\) would be indexed by scenario path (or by information set, to enforce non-anticipativity), and the objective becomes a probability-weighted sum over scenarios rather than a single expectation.

\paragraph{(b) Forward-looking expectation in termination condition (i).}
Condition (i) of (C10) requires \(\mathbb{E}[\Delta t_i\mid\mathcal{F}_t]\), the expected total delay at completion conditional on information at \(t\) — a quantity that itself depends on the future evolution of \(\eta_{i,\cdot}\) and is therefore not a deterministic function of the history up to \(t\). Two standard treatments are available, and the choice affects model class:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Expectation operator with an explicit forecasting model:} retain \(\mathbb{E}[\Delta t_i\mid\mathcal{F}_t]\) as written, with the conditional expectation computed from an EVM-based remaining-duration forecast (e.g., the schedule forecasting methods already cited in Section~\ref{subsubsec:project_state}) treated as a deterministic function of the current state. This keeps (C10)(i) deterministic \emph{given} the forecasting model, at the cost of making the termination rule sensitive to forecast-model choice.
\item \emph{Chance constraint:} replace (C10)(i) with a probabilistic threshold, \(\Pr\!\big[\Delta t_i>D_i^{\mathrm{cap}}/d_i \mid \mathcal{F}_t\big]\geq 1-\epsilon\) for a specified confidence level \(1-\epsilon\), following the chance-constrained programming framework \cite{charnes1959chance}. This is the more conservative OR-standard treatment when the forecast distribution, rather than its point estimate, is considered decision-relevant.
\end{itemize}
Neither treatment is imposed here; both are flagged as the two standard ways to discharge a forward-looking expectation in a constraint, consistent with the solution-agnostic character of this formulation.

\paragraph{(c) Client-caused delay attribution \(\Delta t_i^{\mathrm{client}}\).}
This quantity is treated as exogenous and contractually determined in the original formulation (Section~\ref{subsubsec:project_cashflow}) and is retained as such here; it is listed for completeness, not because it requires a stochastic treatment beyond what the original formulation already specifies.
