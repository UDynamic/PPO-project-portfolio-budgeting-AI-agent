The formulation proceeds across three levels of increasing generality,
all sharing the environment primitives defined in this section.
\textbf{Level~1} (Section~\ref{subsec:level1}) establishes the deterministic
full-foresight MILP whose optimum is a provable upper bound on any causal
policy. \textbf{Level~2} (Section~\ref{subsec:level2}) introduces stochastic
contractor efficiency and characterises the resulting multi-stage stochastic
program, establishing exponential scenario-tree growth. \textbf{Level~3}
(Section~\ref{subsec:level3}) reformulates the intractable stochastic program
as a Constrained Markov Decision Process, yielding the Gymnasium-compatible
environment on which the PPO agent is trained. The three levels form a coherent
arc --- Level~1 defines the performance ceiling, Level~2 explains why it is
unreachable by causal policies, Level~3 provides the tractable path.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Problem Statement and Scope}
\label{subsec:problem_statement}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The problem is operational, not strategic. A portfolio of $n$ contracted
projects is fixed: commitments are signed, start dates are scheduled, and
contracts are parameterised consistently with FIDIC Red, Yellow, and Silver
Book forms \citep{FIDIC2017}. The decision-maker --- a portfolio manager,
board, or programme director --- exercises a single recurring lever at each
discrete planning period $t \in \mathcal{T} = \{1,\ldots,H\}$: the budget
$x_{i,t} \geq 0$ directed to each active project $i$. All outcomes of interest
--- milestone achievement, cash inflows, schedule adherence, and project
termination --- flow through this allocation decision. Portfolio selection is
treated as an exogenous upstream input \citep{Archer1999}.

Four structural assumptions underlie the entire formulation.
(i)~\emph{Independent bilateral contracts}: projects interact only through the
shared cash balance; no resource or workforce sharing occurs, consistent with
standard EPC contracting practice.
(ii)~\emph{Budget as the sole control}: the contractor's internal resource
decisions are absorbed into a stochastic efficiency parameter $\eta_{i,t}$;
the portfolio manager observes how much physical progress a budget allocation
yields, not how it is achieved.
(iii)~\emph{Discrete monthly periods}: $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\ldots,H\}$
reflects the standard monthly governance cycle of portfolio management
(progress reporting, payment certification, performance review).
(iv)~\emph{FIDIC contract mechanics}: milestone payments, advance payment and
recovery, retention, and termination settlement are all calibrated parameters,
not fixed constants, accommodating the full range of particular conditions
encountered in practice.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Sets, Indices, and the Decision Variable}
\label{subsubsec:sets}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6cm, labelwidth=2.4cm]
  \item[$\mathcal{P}$]
    Projects $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, indexed by $i$; all committed at horizon start.
  \item[$\mathcal{T}$]
    Planning periods $\{1,\ldots,H\}$, indexed by $t$ (calendar months).
  \item[$\mathcal{M}_i$]
    Ordered milestones $\{1,\ldots,M_i\}$ for project $i$, indexed by $j$,
    with progress thresholds $\theta_{i,1} < \cdots < \theta_{i,M_i} = 1$.
  \item[$\mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}}$]
    Planned execution window $\{s_i,\ldots,f_i\}$; anchors S-curve
    computations.
  \item[$\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$]
    Projects active at period $t$; fixed at Level~1, endogenous at
    Levels~2--3.
  \item[$\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t)$]
    Milestones of project $i$ certified by period $t$:
    $\{j : \theta_{i,j} \leq P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\}$.
  \item[$x_{i,t} \geq 0$]
    \textbf{Decision variable}: budget allocated to project $i$ at period $t$.
    The period-$t$ action vector is
    $\mathbf{x}_t = (x_{i,t})_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}$.
  \item[$B_t \geq 0$]
    Portfolio cash balance at the start of period $t$; $B_1$ is given.
\end{description}

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Progress and Expenditure Dynamics}
\label{subsubsec:progress}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Budget $x_{i,t}$ converts to physical progress through the efficiency scalar
$\eta_{i,t}$, which absorbs all sources of conversion uncertainty (contractor
productivity, supply chain, weather):
\begin{equation}
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    = P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}
      + \eta_{i,t} \cdot \frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
  \qquad
  E_{i,t} = E_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t},
  \label{eq:progress_rec}
\end{equation}
with $P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}} = E_{i,0} = 0$. Here $\mathrm{BAC}_i$ is the
Budget at Completion --- the total planned contractor expenditure for project
$i$. At Level~1, $\eta_{i,t}$ is replaced by a known deterministic value
$\bar{\eta}_{i,t}$; at Levels~2--3 it is a stochastic primitive realised only
after $x_{i,t}$ is committed.

The planned cumulative expenditure fraction follows a normalised S-curve
\citep{Kenley2009, barraza2004probabilistic} parameterised by shape
parameters $(a_i, b_i)$:
\begin{equation}
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}
    = I_{\tau_{i,t}^c}(a_i, b_i),
  \qquad
  \tau_{i,t} = \frac{t - s_i + 1}{D_i^{\mathrm{plan}}},
  \quad
  \tau_{i,t}^c = \min\{1,\max\{0,\tau_{i,t}\}\},
  \label{eq:scurve}
\end{equation}
where $I_\tau(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the regularized incomplete Beta function and
$D_i^{\mathrm{plan}} = f_i - s_i + 1$ is the planned duration. This profile
is a fixed parameter derived at commitment; it is never a decision variable.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Contractual Cash-Flow Mechanics}
\label{subsubsec:cashflow}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Payment inflows follow FIDIC mechanics \citep{FIDIC2017}. At project
initiation ($t = s_i$), the portfolio manager receives the advance payment
$A_i = \alpha_i \cdot CP_i$, where $\alpha_i$ is the advance ratio and $CP_i
= \mathrm{BAC}_i(1+\pi_i)$ is the contract price with profit margin $\pi_i$.

Milestone $j$ is certified at the first period $t$ at which
$P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \geq \theta_{i,j}$. The net payment received,
after retention withholding at rate $\rho_i$ and advance recovery deduction
at rate $\delta_i^{\mathrm{rec}}$ (commencing once cumulative certified
payments exceed threshold $\psi_i$), is:
\begin{equation}
  R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}
    = \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i (1 - \rho_i)
      - \delta_i^{\mathrm{rec}} \cdot \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i
        \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\left\{
          \textstyle\sum_{k \leq j} \phi_{i,k} \geq \psi_i
        \right\},
  \label{eq:Rnet}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{i,j}$ is the contractual weight of milestone $j$ with
$\sum_j \phi_{i,j} = 1$. This parameterisation is consistent with FIDIC
Sub-Clause~14.2, under which advance recovery deductions commence once
cumulative certified interim payments exceed a specified fraction of the
contract price \citep{FIDIC2017}. Upon project completion
($P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 1$), the accumulated retention
$R_i^{\mathrm{ret}} = \rho_i \cdot CP_i$ is released together with the
final milestone payment. The payment identity
$A_i + \sum_j R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}} + R_i^{\mathrm{ret}} = CP_i$ holds
exactly.

Milestone payments are subject to stochastic timing delays between
certification and receipt at Levels~2--3 \citep{Aibinu2006, Odeh2002}; the
amount is fixed at the contractual net value but the period of receipt is a
random variable. Aggregate period inflow is:
\begin{equation}
  V_t
    = \sum_{i,j} R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}
        \cdot \mathbf{1}\{\text{$(i,j)$ newly certified at $t$}\}
      + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{term}}} R_i^{\mathrm{term}},
  \label{eq:Vt}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{term}}$ collects projects newly terminated at
$t$, with settlement $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ defined in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination} below.

\subsubsection{Performance Monitoring: EVM Signals}
\label{subsubsec:evm}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The Earned Value Management framework \citep{Fleming2010} provides the
diagnostic vocabulary used to monitor contractor performance and to drive
the termination mechanic. The Earned Value at period $t$ is
$\mathrm{EV}_{i,t} = P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i$.
Three derived indices are computed each period:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}
    &= \frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}}{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}},
  \label{eq:SPI} \\
  \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}
    &= \frac{\mathrm{EV}_{i,t}}{E_{i,t}}
     = \frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i}{E_{i,t}},
  \label{eq:CPI} \\
  \mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}
    &= E_{i,t}
       + \frac{\mathrm{BAC}_i - \mathrm{EV}_{i,t}}{\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}}.
  \label{eq:EAC}
\end{align}
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t} < 1$ signals schedule lag; $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t} < 1$
signals cost overrun. $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$ is the forward-looking Estimate
at Completion: it projects the total cost of the project at the current
cost performance rate by scaling remaining planned work by the inverse of
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$, directly analogous to the forecast finish date
computation used in Condition~1 of the termination mechanic
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination}). \citet{Christensen1993} established
that this formulation produces the most accurate and stable cost-at-completion
forecasts, with reliability increasing beyond the 20\% completion mark
\citep{Lipke2003}. All three indices are observable state variables; their
stochastic dynamics at Levels~2--3 are calibrated from empirical EVM datasets
\citep{Acebes2015, Batselier2017}.

\subsubsection{Deviation Penalty}
\label{subsubsec:penalty}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Schedule adherence is priced at every period through a symmetric running
penalty. Let $\delta_{i,t} = P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}$
be the signed progress deviation. The per-period penalty and its cumulative
accumulation are:
\begin{equation}
  c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}
    = \min\!\bigl\{\lambda_i\,|\delta_{i,t}|,\;\Lambda_i \cdot CP_i\bigr\},
  \qquad
  \Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{pen}}
    = \sum_{\tau=s_i}^{t} c_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{dev}},
  \label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_i$ is a per-period penalty weight and $\Lambda_i$ is the
maximum cumulative penalty ratio. The penalty is symmetric in
$|\delta_{i,t}|$: both schedule lag and budget-pushing acceleration are
penalised, discouraging the portfolio manager from over-allocating to a
project at the expense of quality or neighbouring projects
\citep{Merrow2011}. The cap $\Lambda_i \cdot CP_i$ mirrors the contractual
delay-penalty ceiling under FIDIC terms, and $r_i = \Lambda_i \cdot CP_i /
(f_i - s_i)$ gives the implied per-period penalty rate used in the
termination condition below.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Termination Mechanic}
\label{subsubsec:termination}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Termination is an endogenous environment mechanic, not an agent action.
At each period $t$, the environment evaluates two conditions for every
active project $i$. Both are forward-looking trajectory estimates,
symmetric in structure to one another.

\paragraph{Condition 1 --- Schedule tolerance exhausted.}
The forecast finish date, computed by linear extrapolation of remaining
planned duration at the current schedule performance rate, is:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{f}_{i,t}
    = t + \frac{D_i^{\mathrm{plan}} - (t - s_i)}{\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}}.
  \label{eq:fhat}
\end{equation}
Condition~1 activates when the projected delay exceeds the maximum
delay the contract can absorb before the penalty ceiling is reached:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{f}_{i,t} - f_i \;>\; \frac{\Lambda_i}{r_i}.
  \label{eq:term_cond1}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Condition 2 --- Cost overrun trajectory unacceptable.}
The Estimate at Completion $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$ defined in
\eqref{eq:EAC} projects the total project cost at the current cost
performance rate, by scaling remaining planned work by the inverse of
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$ --- directly analogous to the schedule extrapolation
in \eqref{eq:fhat}. Condition~2 activates when the projected total cost
exceeds the project-specific maximum tolerable cost overrun:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{EAC}_{i,t} \;>\; (1 + \mu_i) \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i,
  \label{eq:term_cond2}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_i \geq 0$ is the contractually set maximum cost overrun ratio
for project $i$, fixed at commitment.

\paragraph{Cure-period counter and termination execution.}
When both conditions hold simultaneously, the environment decrements the
project-specific cure-period counter, modelling the FIDIC Sub-Clause~15.1
obligation to allow a recovery interval before termination may be executed
\citep{FIDIC2017, Ndekugri2008}:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}
    = \begin{cases}
        \tau_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{rem}} - 1
          & \text{if \eqref{eq:term_cond1} and \eqref{eq:term_cond2}
                  both hold,} \\
        \tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}}
          & \text{if either condition lapses.}
      \end{cases}
  \label{eq:counter}
\end{equation}
The counter initialises at $\tau_{i,s_i}^{\mathrm{rem}} = \tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}}$,
where $\tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}} \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ is the project-specific
tolerance, fixed at commitment. Termination executes at the period
immediately following
$t_i^{\mathrm{term}} = \min\{t : \tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}} = 0\}$.
The counter is part of the observable portfolio state; the agent cannot
override its dynamics but influences both conditions indirectly through
allocation decisions that drive $\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$ and
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$.

Upon termination at $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$, the net settlement payment is:
\begin{equation}
  R_i^{\mathrm{term}}
    = P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot CP_i (1-\rho_i)
      - \Bigl(A_i
          - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t_i^{\mathrm{term}})}
              \delta_i^{\mathrm{rec}} \cdot \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i
              \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\Bigl\{
                \textstyle\sum_{k \leq j} \phi_{i,k} \geq \psi_i
              \Bigr\}
        \Bigr).
  \label{eq:term_settlement}
\end{equation}
The first term is the value of work delivered net of retention; the second
is the outstanding advance not yet recovered through milestone deductions.
Negative $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ constitutes a net contractor obligation,
recovered from portfolio inflows in subsequent periods consistent with
FIDIC termination clauses \citep{FIDIC2017}.

\subsubsection{Portfolio Cash Balance}
\label{subsubsec:balance}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The portfolio cash balance evolves as:
\begin{equation}
  B_{t+1}
    = B_t
      - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
      + V_t,
  \qquad B_t \geq 0 \quad \forall\,t \in \mathcal{T}.
  \label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
The constraint $B_t \geq 0$ prohibits borrowing; the portfolio operates
under a fixed capital commitment \citep{kavadias2003resource}. The shared
balance $B_t$ is the sole coupling across otherwise independent project
dynamics.

\subsubsection{Observable State and Shared Objective}
\label{subsubsec:objective}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The portfolio state at period $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \Bigl(
    \bigl\{
      P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}},\;
      E_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{EAC}_{i,t},\;
      \delta_{i,t},\;
      \Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{pen}},\;
      \mathrm{status}_{i,t},\;
      \tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}
    \bigr\}_{i \in \mathcal{P}},\;
    B_t
  \Bigr),
  \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{status}_{i,t} \in \{\texttt{active},\, \texttt{completed},\,
\texttt{terminated}\}$. This gives a state dimension of $9n + 1$ per period.
All components are observable; $\eta_{i,t}$ is the sole unobservable
primitive, realised only after $x_{i,t}$ is committed.

The decision-maker maximises discounted net cash flow over the horizon:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\{x_{i,t}\}} \quad
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}
    \!\left(
      V_t
      - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
      - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}
    \right)
  \right],
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
subject to, for all $i \in \mathcal{P}$, $t \in \mathcal{T}$:
\begin{align}
  x_{i,t} &\geq 0,
  \label{eq:nonnegativity} \\
  \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t} &\leq B_t,
  \label{eq:budget} \\
  x_{i,t} &= 0
    \quad \text{if } \mathrm{status}_{i,t} \neq \texttt{active}.
  \label{eq:inactive}
\end{align}
There is no terminal sum. Completion incentives are embedded structurally:
$R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}$ and $R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}$ enter $V_t$ at the
final certification period; $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ (possibly negative) enters
at termination, creating a natural deterrent against allowing project
performance to deteriorate to the termination threshold. The expectation
$\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ collapses to a deterministic evaluation at Level~1 and
is retained at Levels~2 and~3.

\subsection{Level~1: Deterministic Full-Foresight MILP}
\label{subsec:level1}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Setting $\eta_{i,t} = \bar{\eta}_{i,t}$ (known) renders $S_t$ a
deterministic function of $\mathbf{x}_t$, so $\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$,
milestone certification times, and termination periods $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$
are all computable ex ante. Under deterministic efficiency, the trajectories
of $\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$, and
the counter $\tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}$ are fully determined before solving;
both termination conditions \eqref{eq:term_cond1}--\eqref{eq:term_cond2} and
the counter dynamics \eqref{eq:counter} are parameter-verifiable, so
termination periods $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ and their settlements
$R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ enter the MILP as fixed parameters rather than
optimisation constraints.

Introducing the certification binary $u_{i,j,t} \in \{0,1\}$ ($=1$ iff
milestone $j$ of project $i$ certified by period $t$) and activity indicator
$\mathrm{act}_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ (fixed parameter), the stochastic
objective~\eqref{eq:objective} collapses to:

\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:L1}
\begin{align}
  \max \quad
  & \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}\!\left(
      V_t^{\mathrm{det}}
      - \sum_{i} \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\, x_{i,t}
      - \sum_{i} \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\, c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}
    \right)
  \label{eq:L1_obj} \\[4pt]
  \text{s.t.} \quad
  B_{t+1}
    &= B_t - \sum_i \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\,x_{i,t} + V_t^{\mathrm{det}},
    \quad B_t \geq 0,
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L1_balance} \\
  \sum_i \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\, x_{i,t}
    &\leq B_t,
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L1_budget} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &= P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}
       + \bar{\eta}_{i,t}\,\tfrac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
    \quad P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \leq 1,
    && \forall\, i,t
  \label{eq:L1_progress} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &\geq \theta_{i,j}\,u_{i,j,t} - \mathbf{M}(1 - u_{i,j,t}),
    && \forall\, i,j,t
  \label{eq:L1_cert_lb} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &\leq \theta_{i,j} + \mathbf{M}\,u_{i,j,t},
    \quad u_{i,j,t} \geq u_{i,j,t-1},
    \quad u_{i,j+1,t} \leq u_{i,j,t},
    && \forall\, i,j,t
  \label{eq:L1_cert_logic} \\
  V_t^{\mathrm{det}}
    &= \sum_{i,j} R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}\,(u_{i,j,t} - u_{i,j,t-1})
       + \sum_i R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}\,(u_{i,M_i,t} - u_{i,M_i,t-1})
       \notag \\
    &\quad
       + \sum_i A_i \cdot \mathbf{1}\{t = s_i\}
       + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{term}}}
           R_i^{\mathrm{term}} \cdot \mathbf{1}\{t = t_i^{\mathrm{term}}\},
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L1_cashflow} \\
  \delta_{i,t}
    &= P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}
     = d_{i,t}^+ - d_{i,t}^-,
    \quad d_{i,t}^+, d_{i,t}^- \geq 0,
    && \forall\, i,t
  \label{eq:L1_dev_split} \\
  c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}
    &\geq \lambda_i(d_{i,t}^+ + d_{i,t}^-),
    \quad c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}} \leq \Lambda_i \cdot CP_i,
    \quad c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}} \geq 0,
    && \forall\, i,t
  \label{eq:L1_penalty_lin} \\
  x_{i,t}
    &= 0 \quad \text{if } \mathrm{act}_{i,t} = 0,
    && \forall\, i,t
  \label{eq:L1_inactive} \\
  x_{i,t} &\geq 0,
    \quad u_{i,j,t} \in \{0,1\},
    \quad u_{i,j,0} = P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 0.
  \label{eq:L1_domains}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

The big-$M$ constant is $\mathbf{M} = \max_{i,j}\theta_{i,j}$.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:L1_cert_lb}--\eqref{eq:L1_cert_logic} implement exact
if-and-only-if certification logic with monotone, ordered binary activation.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:L1_dev_split}--\eqref{eq:L1_penalty_lin} linearise the
absolute-value and cap in \eqref{eq:penalty}: the maximising objective drives
$c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}$ to the tighter of the two bounds, recovering the
exact symmetric penalty. The difference $(u_{i,j,t} - u_{i,j,t-1})$ in
\eqref{eq:L1_cashflow} equals one in the sole period a milestone is first
certified, linearising the payment trigger without additional binaries.

\paragraph{Upper bound.}
Let $Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$ denote the Level~1 optimum. For any causal policy
$\pi$ feasible under uncertainty, $\sup_\pi \mathbb{E}[Z^\pi] \leq
Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$: every feasible causal allocation sequence is also
feasible in the Level~1 program solved on the realised trajectory, so the
full-foresight optimum dominates in expectation. The gap
$Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}} - Z^\pi$ quantifies the cost of uncertainty and anchors
the empirical comparison in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Level~2: Stochastic Extension and Intractability}
\label{subsec:level2}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

When $\eta_{i,t}$ is replaced by a random variable --- as it must be to
reflect reality --- the progress recursion \eqref{eq:progress_rec} becomes
stochastic and $S_t$ can no longer be computed ex ante. The MILP of
Section~\ref{subsec:level1} becomes a multi-stage stochastic program
\citep{Birge2011}: at each period $t$, the allocation $\mathbf{x}_t$ must
be selected as a function of the history $\mathcal{H}_t =
(S_1, \mathbf{x}_1, \ldots, S_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_{t-1}, S_t)$, and the
optimisation must account for all future realisations of $\eta_{i,t}$.

The scenario-tree representation of this program grows exponentially. If
$\eta_{i,t}$ takes $K$ discrete values at each of $H$ periods for each of
$n$ projects, the scenario tree has $K^{nH}$ leaf nodes. For a portfolio of
ten projects over a 24-month horizon with five efficiency scenarios per
project per period, this is $5^{240}$ leaf nodes --- a number that renders
exact stochastic programming computationally intractable regardless of
solver capability. The intractability is not a function of the MILP
structure; it is intrinsic to the multi-stage stochastic formulation. Even
replacing the binary milestone variables with continuous relaxations does
not change the exponential scaling in $n$ and $H$.

This intractability is not merely a computational inconvenience. It
establishes a fundamental barrier: no exact method can compute the
multi-stage stochastic optimal policy at realistic portfolio scales. This
barrier motivates the Level~3 reformulation, which trades exact optimality
for tractability by reformulating the problem as a sequential
decision-process solved through sampled interaction rather than exhaustive
scenario enumeration.

It is worth noting that termination timing, which is a fixed parameter at
Level~1, becomes an endogenous stochastic outcome at Level~2: since
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$, and $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$ all
depend on the realised $\eta_{i,\tau}$ trajectory, the periods at which
Conditions~\eqref{eq:term_cond1}--\eqref{eq:term_cond2} activate and the
counter $\tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}$ reaches zero are random variables. This
further compounds the scenario-tree explosion, since each termination event
creates a structural change in the active project set $\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$
that bifurcates the tree.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Level~3: Constrained MDP and RL Agent}
\label{subsec:level3}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The Constrained Markov Decision Process \citep{Altman1999} provides the
tractable reformulation. Rather than optimising over all scenario-tree
paths simultaneously, the CMDP formalises the problem as a sequential
interaction between a policy and an environment: at each period $t$, the
policy observes the current portfolio state $S_t$, selects an allocation
$\mathbf{x}_t$, the environment transitions stochastically to $S_{t+1}$,
and a reward is received. The policy is optimised through repeated
interaction with the environment, using sampled trajectories rather than
exhaustive scenario trees.

\paragraph{Mapping from MILP to CMDP.}
The mapping from the Level~1 MILP to the Level~3 CMDP is exact in
structure:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6cm]
  \item \textbf{Action space}: the decision variables $\{x_{i,t}\}$ become
    the continuous action vector $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}|}_{\geq 0}$.
  \item \textbf{Observation space}: the portfolio state $S_t$ defined in
    \eqref{eq:state}, with dimension $9n + 1$.
  \item \textbf{Constrained action space}: the budget constraint
    \eqref{eq:budget} and inactivity constraint \eqref{eq:inactive} define
    the feasible action set at each period, implementing the hard liquidity
    constraint that the agent cannot violate.
  \item \textbf{Reward signal}: the per-period term inside the expectation
    in \eqref{eq:objective}, discounted by $\gamma^{t-1}$.
  \item \textbf{Transition dynamics}: the stochastic progress recursion
    \eqref{eq:progress_rec}, cash-flow mechanics
    \eqref{eq:Rnet}--\eqref{eq:Vt}, EVM signal updates
    \eqref{eq:SPI}--\eqref{eq:EAC}, deviation penalty \eqref{eq:penalty},
    termination counter \eqref{eq:counter}, and balance recursion
    \eqref{eq:balance} together define the environment transition kernel
    $\mathcal{T}(S_{t+1} \mid S_t, \mathbf{x}_t)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Gymnasium environment.}
The CMDP is implemented as a configurable Gymnasium-compatible environment
\citep{Towers2024}. All project and portfolio parameters (Table~\ref{tab:params})
are passed as a configuration dictionary at initialisation, making the
environment fully parameterisable across contract types, portfolio sizes,
and planning horizons without code modification. The stochastic efficiency
$\eta_{i,t}$ is drawn each period from a project-specific distribution
calibrated from empirical EVM datasets \citep{Acebes2015, Batselier2017}.
Milestone payment timing delays are drawn from project-specific stochastic
delay distributions \citep{Aibinu2006}. The environment enforces the budget
constraint as a hard projection of the agent's action vector onto the
feasible simplex before applying the transition, ensuring constraint
satisfaction by construction.

\paragraph{PPO agent and training protocol.}
A Proximal Policy Optimisation agent \citep{Schulman2017} is trained on the
environment for its stability properties in continuous action spaces and its
established use in operations management domains \citep{Giannoccaro2002,
Peng2021}. Domain randomisation \citep{Tobin2017} --- randomising all
parameters in Table~\ref{tab:params} within calibrated ranges during
training --- ensures the learned policy generalises across project types,
contract structures, and market conditions rather than overfitting to a
single parameterisation. Walk-forward validation \citep{Cerqueira2020} is
used as the empirical evaluation protocol to guard against data leakage
given the time-series structure of project cash-flow data.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The PPO agent is evaluated against three baselines in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}: the Level~1 MILP oracle
($Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$, the theoretical upper bound), a two-stage stochastic
program representing the best tractable exact method, and a rule-based
proportional heuristic representing current practice. The gap between the
PPO agent and the MILP oracle quantifies the cost of uncertainty; the gap
between the PPO agent and the rule-based heuristic quantifies the practical
value of the learned policy.
