\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Overview}
\label{subsec:overview}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

This section develops the formal problem structure for portfolio-level budget
allocation in contracted project portfolios. The formulation proceeds in three
levels of increasing generality, each building directly on the preceding one.

\textbf{Level~1 --- Deterministic Full-Foresight Linear Program.}
All contractor performance parameters, expenditure trajectories, and payment
timing are treated as known with certainty. The resulting program is a pure
linear program (LP) in the continuous budget allocation variables. Because no
real decision-maker possesses this information ex ante, the Level~1 optimum
constitutes a theoretical upper bound on portfolio performance: no
implementable policy can exceed it. This upper bound anchors the empirical
comparison in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\textbf{Level~2 --- Stochastic Extension and Intractability.}
Deterministic performance parameters are replaced by stochastic processes
consistent with the empirical EVM literature. The resulting multi-stage
stochastic program is formally characterized, and the combinatorial growth of
its scenario tree with the number of projects and planning periods is
established. This intractability argument motivates the reformulation in
Level~3.

\textbf{Level~3 --- Constrained Markov Decision Process.}
The stochastic program of Level~2 is reformulated as a Constrained Markov
Decision Process (CMDP). An explicit mapping is provided from LP elements to
MDP primitives: decision variables map to actions, constraint parameters map to
state variables, the objective maps to the reward function, and feasibility
constraints define the constrained action space. The CMDP is implemented as a
configurable, Gymnasium-compatible synthetic environment; a Proximal Policy
Optimization (PPO) agent trained on this environment serves as the primary
computational demonstration of the framework's utility.

The three levels are not alternative models of the same problem. They are a
coherent modeling arc: Level~1 defines the problem's theoretical ceiling,
Level~2 characterizes why that ceiling is computationally unreachable, and
Level~3 provides the tractable reformulation that makes the problem solvable at
realistic scale. The remainder of this section develops each level in turn,
preceded by the shared problem primitives on which all three levels draw.

The formulation integrates domain knowledge from project management and
operations research: S-curve expenditure dynamics
\cite{kenley1986relationship,barraza2004probabilistic}, Earned Value Management
(EVM) performance tracking \cite{fleming2016earned}, milestone-based payment
structures consistent with FIDIC contract forms
\cite{fidic2017silver,cidb2015construction,eggers2019lump}, and the theory of
investment under uncertainty as it applies to abandonment options
\cite{dixit1994investment}. The LP of Level~1 is situated within the
stochastic programming tradition \cite{birge2011introduction}; the CMDP of
Level~3 is situated within the constrained MDP literature
\cite{altman1999constrained}, framed as a direct descendant of Bellman's
dynamic programming and stochastic control theory.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Scope and Positioning}
\label{subsec:scope}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The problem addressed here is operational, not strategic. The literature on
project portfolio management has devoted considerable attention to the
\emph{selection} problem: given a candidate set of projects and a capital
budget, which subset should the organization commit to
\cite{archer1999project,kavadias2003resource}? This work takes the selection
decision as given. A portfolio of contracted projects exists; commitments have
been signed; and the relevant decision-maker --- a portfolio manager or
governing board --- faces a recurring operational question: how should the
available budget be allocated across active projects at each discrete planning
period?

This is the budget allocation control problem, and it is the sole focus of the
formulation. The decision-maker exercises one lever: the amount of budget
directed to each active project at each period. All strategic and operational
outcomes of interest --- milestone achievement, cash flow balance, schedule
adherence, and ultimate project success or termination --- flow through this
single allocation decision. The formulation makes this causal chain explicit
and embeds it within a sequential decision-making framework.

The model is deliberately general. Every structural element --- the number of
projects, the milestone structure, the S-curve shape parameters, the advance
payment and retention ratios, the performance response distribution, the
deviation penalty weight, and the termination settlement mechanics --- is
parameterized rather than fixed. Sector-specific instantiation is achieved
through empirical calibration of these parameters, not through changes to the
model structure. The calibration used in this work draws on anonymized data
from two major EPC contractors, supplemented by World Bank ICR data
\cite{worldbank2023icr} and infrastructure cost overrun datasets
\cite{flyvbjerg2003underestimating}. The general structure is applicable across
construction, software development, defense contracting, and other
project-intensive sectors \cite{kenley1986relationship,christensen1993empirical}.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{subsec:assumptions}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Four structural choices underlie the entire formulation. Each is stated here
with its justification, because they determine the form of every constraint and
variable that follows.

\paragraph{A portfolio is an aggregation of independent bilateral contracts.}
Each project in the portfolio corresponds to a separate contract between the
client and a distinct contractor. Projects do not share resources, workforces,
or contractual obligations within the formulation; interaction occurs only
through the shared portfolio budget constraint. This independence structure is
consistent with standard EPC contracting practice and permits the portfolio
problem to be decomposed into project-level dynamics aggregated at the
portfolio level. It is also the structure that makes the LP of Level~1
tractable: the coupling across projects enters only through the single budget
constraint at each period.

\paragraph{The resource allocation problem reduces to budget allocation.}
Within each project, the contractor controls workforce, materials, equipment,
and subcontracting. From the portfolio manager's perspective, the only
instrument available is the budget directed to each project. The contractor's
internal response to that budget --- the efficiency with which it is converted
into physical progress --- is modeled as a performance parameter, stochastic at
Level~2 and treated as known at Level~1. This reduction of the resource
allocation problem to a scalar budget allocation per project per period is both
a modeling choice and a reflection of the actual decision authority of a
portfolio-level governing body.

\paragraph{Time is discrete, with periods representing calendar months.}
The planning horizon $\mathcal{T} = \{1, \ldots, H\}$ is indexed by discrete
periods. Monthly periods are adopted because the key governance events in
portfolio management --- progress reporting, payment certification, performance
review meetings --- are conventionally held on a monthly cycle. The LP of
Level~1 optimizes over the full horizon simultaneously; the CMDP of Level~3
operates period by period, with decisions made sequentially as new information
is revealed.

\paragraph{Contract mechanics are calibrated to FIDIC forms.}
The milestone-based payment structure, advance payment and recovery mechanism,
retention and release, and termination settlement mechanics are all specified to
be consistent with FIDIC Red, Yellow, and Silver Book contract forms
\cite{fidic2017silver}. This anchors the formulation in the dominant
international EPC contracting framework and ensures that the model parameters
have direct empirical counterparts in contractor financial data. Where FIDIC
allows variation by agreement (e.g., the advance payment ratio, the retention
percentage, the deviation penalty weight), these are treated as
project-specific calibrated parameters rather than fixed constants.

\subsection{Problem Primitives}
\label{subsec:primitives}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

This subsection establishes the complete notation, project-level parameters,
state variables, transition dynamics, and portfolio aggregation structure on
which all three formulation levels draw. The organization mirrors the
environment's ontology: objects are defined first (projects and their
parameters), then their state, then the dynamics that move state forward, then
the portfolio composition, and finally the shared objective. Nothing introduced
here is level-specific; every symbol defined below appears without redefinition
across Level~1, Level~2, and Level~3.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Sets and Indices}
\label{subsubsec:sets}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

All symbols introduced in subsequent subsections are indexed over the sets
defined here. This subsection is exhaustive: every set, index, and structured
collection that appears anywhere in the formulation is declared below.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Primary index sets.}

\begin{description}
  \item[$\mathcal{P} = \{1, \ldots, n\}$]
    Set of projects in the portfolio, indexed by $i$. All projects are
    committed at the start of the planning horizon; no new projects enter
    mid-horizon.

  \item[$\mathcal{T} = \{1, \ldots, H\}$]
    Set of discrete planning periods, indexed by $t$, each representing one
    calendar month.

  \item[$\mathcal{M}_i = \{1, \ldots, M_i\}$]
    Ordered set of contractual milestones for project $i$, indexed by $j$.
    Milestones are ordered by their cumulative progress thresholds:
    $\theta_{i,1} < \theta_{i,2} < \cdots < \theta_{i,M_i} = 1$.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Derived and dynamic sets.}

\begin{description}
  \item[$\mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}} = \{t : s_i \leq t \leq f_i\}$]
    Planned execution window of project $i$, spanning its planned start
    $s_i$ through its planned finish $f_i$. All S-curve and deviation
    computations are anchored to this window.

  \item[$\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}} \subseteq \mathcal{P}$]
    Subset of projects active at period $t$. In Level~1, this set is
    determined ex ante from the known efficiency trajectories
    $\{\bar{\eta}_{i,t}\}$ and is a fixed parameter. In Level~2 and
    Level~3, it is endogenous, evolving with realized status transitions.

  \item[$\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t) \subseteq \mathcal{M}_i$]
    Subset of milestones for project $i$ certified up to and including
    period $t$:
    \[
      \mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t)
        = \bigl\{j \in \mathcal{M}_i
            : \theta_{i,j} \leq P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}\bigr\}.
    \]
    This set grows monotonically over time and governs the release of
    milestone payments and advance recovery deductions.

  \item[$\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{comp}} \subseteq \mathcal{P}$,\;
        $\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{term}} \subseteq \mathcal{P}$]
    Subsets of projects that have reached \texttt{completed} or
    \texttt{terminated} status, respectively, by the end of the planning
    horizon. These partition $\mathcal{P}$ together with
    $\mathcal{P}_H^{\mathrm{active}}$.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Decision variable.}

\begin{description}
  \item[$x_{i,t} \geq 0$]
    Budget allocated to project $i \in \mathcal{P}$ at period
    $t \in \mathcal{T}$. This is the sole decision variable of the
    portfolio manager. The action at period $t$ is the vector
    $\mathbf{x}_t = (x_{i,t})_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}$.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Portfolio-level scalar.}

\begin{description}
  \item[$B_t \geq 0$]
    Available portfolio cash balance at the beginning of period $t$.
    $B_1$ is given as an initial condition. The balance evolves through
    the portfolio aggregation dynamics defined in
    Section~\ref{subsubsec:portfolio_aggregation}.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Planned Expenditure Profile.}
The planned cumulative expenditure of project $i$ follows a normalized S-curve
\cite{kenley1986relationship,barraza2004probabilistic}, parameterized by
$(a_i, b_i)$ from Table~\ref{tab:params-schedule-contract}. Define the
normalized planned time at period $t$ as:
\[
  \tau_{i,t} = \frac{t - s_i + 1}{D_i^{\mathrm{plan}}},
  \qquad t \in \mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}},
\]
with the clamped value $\tau_{i,t}^{c} = \min\{1,\max\{0,\tau_{i,t}\}\}$
handling periods outside the planned window. The planned cumulative expenditure
fraction and its period increment are:
\[
  F_i(\tau) = I_{\tau}(a_i, b_i),
  \qquad
  \bar{E}_{i,t} = \mathrm{BAC}_i \cdot F_i(\tau_{i,t}^{c}),
  \qquad
  \Delta\bar{E}_{i,t} = \bar{E}_{i,t} - \bar{E}_{i,t-1},
\]
where $I_{\tau}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the regularized incomplete Beta function.
Under the nominal plan, planned cumulative progress tracks planned expenditure
fraction:
\[
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}} = F_i(\tau_{i,t}^{c}).
\]
This profile is a fixed project parameter derived at commitment from
$(a_i, b_i, s_i, f_i, \mathrm{BAC}_i)$. It is not a decision variable; it
serves as the baseline against which actual execution is measured in every
deviation and EVM computation that follows.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{State Variables}
\label{subsubsec:state_variables}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

State variables describe the condition of each project and the portfolio at the
end of each period. They are grouped into two tiers that reflect the
environment's ontology: project-level states capture the execution history and
performance of individual projects; the portfolio-level state aggregates these
into the quantities relevant to the allocation decision.

\paragraph{Project-level state.}
For each $i \in \mathcal{P}$ and $t \in \mathcal{T}$:

\begin{description}
  \item[$P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \in [0,1]$]
    Cumulative actual progress: the fraction of project scope physically
    completed at the end of period $t$. Initialized at
    $P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 0$.

  \item[$E_{i,t} \geq 0$]
    Cumulative actual expenditure on project $i$ up to and including
    period $t$. Initialized at $E_{i,0} = 0$.

  \item[$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$]
    Schedule Performance Index:
    \[
      \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}
        = \frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}}{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}},
      \qquad P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}} > 0.
    \]
    Values below unity indicate schedule underperformance; values above
    unity indicate acceleration ahead of plan.

  \item[$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$]
    Cost Performance Index:
    \[
      \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}
        = \frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i}{E_{i,t}},
      \qquad E_{i,t} > 0.
    \]
    Values below unity indicate cost overrun relative to work performed.

  \item[$\delta_{i,t}$]
    Signed progress deviation:
    \[
      \delta_{i,t} = P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}.
    \]
    Negative values indicate schedule lag; positive values indicate
    acceleration beyond the planned profile.

  \item[$\Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{pen}} \geq 0$]
    Cumulative deviation penalty accrued up to period $t$. Defined and
    computed in Section~\ref{subsubsec:dynamics}.

  \item[$\mathrm{status}_{i,t} \in
      \{\texttt{active},\,\texttt{completed},\,\texttt{terminated}\}$]
    Categorical project status at the end of period $t$. Transitions are
    governed by the progress recursion and the termination conditions
    defined in Sections~\ref{subsubsec:dynamics}
    and~\ref{subsubsec:termination}.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Portfolio-level state.}
The portfolio state at period $t$ is the composition of all project states
together with the shared cash balance:
\[
  S_t
    = \Bigl(
        \bigl\{
          P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}},\;
          E_{i,t},\;
          \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\;
          \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t},\;
          \delta_{i,t},\;
          \Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{pen}},\;
          \mathrm{status}_{i,t}
        \bigr\}_{i \in \mathcal{P}},\;
        B_t
      \Bigr).
\]
The dimension of $S_t$ scales linearly with $n$. The cash balance $B_t$ is
the sole portfolio-level quantity that couples project dynamics: it is the
shared resource over which allocation decisions are contested at each period,
and its evolution is governed by the aggregation defined in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:portfolio_aggregation}.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Transition Dynamics}
\label{subsubsec:dynamics}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The transition dynamics determine how the portfolio state $S_t$ evolves to
$S_{t+1}$ given the allocation decision $\mathbf{x}_t$. Three mechanisms
govern this transition: the conversion of allocated budget into physical
progress, the contractual cash-flow mechanics that translate progress into
payments, and the running deviation penalty that prices schedule adherence.

\paragraph{Budget-to-Progress Conversion.}
Allocated budget $x_{i,t}$ is converted to a progress increment through an
efficiency parameter:
\[
  \Delta P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    = \eta_{i,t} \cdot \frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
\]
where $\eta_{i,t}$ is the \emph{budget-to-progress efficiency} for project $i$
at period $t$. This scalar captures all sources of conversion uncertainty ---
workforce productivity, supply chain conditions, technical difficulty, and
weather --- that determine how much physical progress a given budget expenditure
yields in practice.

\textit{Treatment across levels.} In Level~1, $\eta_{i,t}$ is replaced by a
known deterministic value $\bar{\eta}_{i,t}$ calibrated from the project's
planned performance profile; under the nominal plan, $\bar{\eta}_{i,t} = 1$
implies that expenditure tracks the S-curve exactly. In Level~2 and Level~3,
$\eta_{i,t}$ is a random primitive realized only after $x_{i,t}$ is committed,
with a distribution calibrated from empirical EVM data.

The project-level state recursion follows directly:
\[
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    = P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}
      + \eta_{i,t} \cdot \frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
  \qquad
  E_{i,t} = E_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t},
\]
with $P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 0$ and $E_{i,0} = 0$.

\paragraph{Contractual Cash-Flow Mechanics.}
Project cash flows consist of outflows (budget allocations $x_{i,t}$) and
inflows (contractual payments). The payment structure is consistent with FIDIC
contract forms \cite{fidic2017silver} and comprises three mechanisms.

\textit{Advance Payment.}
At project initiation (period $s_i$), the client disburses:
\[
  A_i = \alpha_i \cdot CP_i.
\]
The advance payment is a contractual timing adjustment, not a financing
instrument: it carries no interest obligation and is recovered progressively
through deductions from subsequent milestone payments.

\textit{Advance Recovery.}
The recovery deduction applied at milestone $j$ is proportional to the
milestone payment weight:
\[
  \Delta A_{i,j} = \phi_{i,j} \cdot A_i.
\]
This ensures the full advance is recovered by the time all milestones are
certified, regardless of project duration.

\textit{Milestone Certification and Net Payment.}
Milestone $j \in \mathcal{M}_i$ is certified at the first period $t$ such
that $P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \geq \theta_{i,j}$. On certification, the
gross milestone payment, net payment after retention, and net payment after
both retention and advance recovery are:
\[
  R_{i,j} = \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i \cdot (1 - \rho_i),
  \qquad
  R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}} = R_{i,j} - \Delta A_{i,j}.
\]
Retention and advance recovery operate independently: recovery deductions are
applied to gross payments prior to retention. The accounting identity across
all payment flows is:
\[
  A_i
    + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}_i} R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}
    + R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}
    = CP_i,
\]
where $R_i^{\mathrm{ret}} = \rho_i \cdot CP_i$ is the total retention
accumulated across milestones and released at completion.

\textit{Final Payment and Retention Release.}
Upon completion ($P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 1$), the final net inflow is:
\[
  R_i^{\mathrm{final}} = R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}} + R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}.
\]
No separate lump-sum delay penalty appears here; schedule adherence is enforced
entirely through the continuous running deviation penalty defined next. The
retention release and final milestone payment together constitute the natural
completion incentive embedded in the payment structure.

\paragraph{Symmetric Running Deviation Penalty.}
Schedule adherence is priced continuously through a symmetric running penalty
assessed at every period $t \in \mathcal{T}$. The per-period penalty for
project $i$ is:
\[
  c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}
    = \min\!\left\{
        \lambda_i \cdot \lvert \delta_{i,t} \rvert,\;
        \Lambda_i \cdot CP_i
      \right\},
\]
where $\delta_{i,t} = P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}$
is the signed progress deviation, $\lambda_i \geq 0$ is the per-unit penalty
weight, and $\Lambda_i$ is the cap as a fraction of $CP_i$.

Three design properties of this specification are explicit.

\textit{Symmetry.} The penalty applies to $|\delta_{i,t}|$, not to
$\max\{0, -\delta_{i,t}\}$. Both schedule lag and acceleration are penalized.
This discourages the decision-maker from pushing projects ahead of plan, which
consumes budget inefficiently and risks scope or quality degradation
\cite{merrow2011industrial}.

\textit{Continuity.} The penalty is assessed every period, not only at
contractual completion. This provides a continuously updating signal that feeds
the termination assessment in Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination}: a project
whose cumulative penalty grows monotonically signals persistent
underperformance before any contractual deadline is breached.

\textit{Calibration to contractual terms.} The weight $\lambda_i$ is calibrated
so that the cumulative penalty at the planned finish date, under a sustained
delay equal to the contractual limit, equals $\Lambda_i \cdot CP_i$. This
connects the running signal to the actual contractual delay-penalty exposure
assigned by FIDIC terms \cite{fidic2017silver}.

The cumulative deviation penalty up to period $t$ is:
\[
  \Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{pen}}
    = \sum_{\tau = s_i}^{t} c_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{dev}}.
\]
This quantity enters both the objective function as a running cost and the
termination condition as a trigger threshold.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Termination Conditions and Settlement}
\label{subsubsec:termination}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

A project transitions from \texttt{active} to \texttt{terminated} at the first
period $t$ at which both of the following conditions hold simultaneously:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Penalty saturation.} The cumulative deviation penalty has
    reached or exceeded the contractual ceiling:
    \[
      \Delta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{pen}} \geq \kappa_i \cdot \Lambda_i \cdot CP_i.
    \]
    This indicates that the project has exhausted its schedule tolerance and
    the client has lost contractual leverage over the contractor.

  \item \textbf{Confirmed allocation floor.} The portfolio manager has
    allocated no more than the minimum termination allocation $m_i^{0}$ for
    $k$ consecutive periods:
    \[
      x_{i,\tilde{t}} \leq m_i^{0}
        \quad \forall\,\tilde{t} \in \{t - k + 1, \ldots, t\}.
    \]
    Holding allocation at $m_i^{0}$ is the mechanism by which the portfolio
    manager signals economic abandonment: it is the lowest level at which
    contractual obligations are serviced without active investment. Sustaining
    this floor for $k$ periods confirms that the decision to cease investment
    is deliberate rather than transient.
\end{enumerate}

The role of $m_i^{0}$ is therefore specific and bounded: it is not a
performance-recovery instrument, but the floor allocation required to hold a
project in a legally serviceable state while the termination conditions are
evaluated. It has no other role in the formulation.

The simultaneous satisfaction of both conditions ensures termination occurs only
when the project is both contractually disadvantageous (condition~1) and
operationally abandoned (condition~2).

\paragraph{Termination Settlement.}
Upon termination at period $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$, the net settlement payment is:
\begin{align*}
  V_i^{\mathrm{term}}
    &= P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot CP_i, \\
  A_i^{\mathrm{outstanding}}
    &= A_i - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t_i^{\mathrm{term}})}
        \Delta A_{i,j}, \\
  R_i^{\mathrm{term}}
    &= V_i^{\mathrm{term}}(1 - \rho_i) - A_i^{\mathrm{outstanding}}.
\end{align*}
If $R_i^{\mathrm{term}} < 0$, the outstanding advance payment balance
constitutes a net obligation settled from available portfolio cash inflows ahead
of other commitments, consistent with FIDIC termination clauses
\cite{fidic2017silver}.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Portfolio Aggregation}
\label{subsubsec:portfolio_aggregation}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The portfolio $\mathcal{P}$ aggregates $n$ projects whose individual dynamics
are coupled through the shared cash balance $B_t$. The cash balance evolves as:
\[
  B_{t+1}
    = B_t
      - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
      + V_t,
  \qquad \forall\, t \in \mathcal{T},
\]
where the aggregate inflow $V_t$ collects all contractual payments received at
period $t$:
\[
  V_t
    = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}_i}
        R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}
        \cdot \mathbf{1}\bigl\{
          \text{milestone } (i,j) \text{ newly certified at } t
        \bigr\}
      + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{term}}_t} R_i^{\mathrm{term}},
\]
with $\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{term}}_t$ the set of projects newly terminated at
period $t$. The constraint $B_t \geq 0$ is enforced at all periods; no
borrowing or credit facility is available. This hard constraint eliminates the
soft budget dynamics present in formulations that allow internal credit and is
consistent with the governance structure of a portfolio-level board operating
under a fixed capital commitment \cite{kavadias2003resource}.

The portfolio state $S_t$ defined in Section~\ref{subsubsec:state_variables}
therefore evolves as a deterministic function of $\mathbf{x}_t$ under Level~1,
and as a controlled stochastic process under Level~2 and Level~3. The linear
scaling of $\dim(S_t)$ with $n$, combined with the full-horizon coupling
through $B_t$, characterizes the computational complexity of the portfolio
allocation problem and motivates the intractability argument in Level~2.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Objective}
\label{subsubsec:objective}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The decision-maker seeks to maximize the discounted cumulative net cash flow
over the planning horizon:
\[
  \max_{\{x_{i,t}\}}
    \quad
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[
      \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}
        \!\left(
          V_t
          - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
          - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}
              c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{dev}}
        \right)
    \right].
\]
There is no separate terminal sum. Completion incentives are embedded
structurally: the retention amount $R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}$ and the final milestone
payment $R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}$ enter through $V_t$ at the period in which
completion is certified. Projects that terminate early contribute
$R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ to $V_t$ at the termination period, which may be
negative, creating a natural deterrent against premature abandonment.

In Level~1, the expectation $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ collapses to a deterministic
evaluation under $\bar{\eta}_{i,t}$; the stochastic operator is retained here
to preserve continuity across all three levels.

The constraints on $\{x_{i,t}\}$ are:
\begin{align}
  x_{i,t} &\geq 0,
    && \forall\, i \in \mathcal{P},\; t \in \mathcal{T},
    \label{eq:nonnegativity} \\
  \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t} &\leq B_t,
    && \forall\, t \in \mathcal{T},
    \label{eq:budget} \\
  x_{i,t} &\geq m_i^{0}
    \cdot \mathbf{1}\bigl\{
      \mathrm{status}_{i,t} = \texttt{active}
    \bigr\},
    && \forall\, i \in \mathcal{P},\; t \in \mathcal{T}.
    \label{eq:floor}
\end{align}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:floor} enforces the contractual floor on active projects:
allocations must not fall below $m_i^{0}$ while a project remains active, as
doing so would constitute a client default on contractual obligations. Once
$\mathrm{status}_{i,t} \in \{\texttt{completed}, \texttt{terminated}\}$,
the indicator is zero and the floor constraint is vacuous. The floor therefore
serves exclusively as the mechanism that holds the project in a serviceable
state while the termination conditions of
Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination} are evaluated; it has no other role in
the feasible set.

\medskip
These primitives --- index sets, project parameters including the planned
expenditure profile, project and portfolio state variables, transition dynamics,
termination conditions, portfolio aggregation, and the shared objective ---
form the complete foundation from which each formulation level is constructed.
Level~1 specializes them under deterministic full foresight; Level~2
reintroduces stochasticity explicitly; Level~3 reformulates the resulting
stochastic program as a CMDP.
