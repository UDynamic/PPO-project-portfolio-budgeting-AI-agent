\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

\subsection{Management Problem and Portfolio Setting}
\label{subsec:setting}

We consider a finite-horizon, discrete-time budget management problem for a committed portfolio of projects under schedule, cost, and funding uncertainty. The decision maker does not select which projects to undertake; the portfolio composition is fixed. Instead, the task is to allocate a constrained budget over periods \(t = 1,\dots,H\) among an existing set of projects \(P\), while monitoring progress and deciding when to activate corrective interventions. Each project consumes resources gradually, generates revenue through milestone payments, and may suffer delays or cost overruns. The management objective is to maximise a flexible composite measure of portfolio value that reflects the organisation's strategic priorities—rewarding profitable completion, penalising excessive risk, and discouraging the wasteful continuation of underperforming projects.

\subsection{Portfolio and Project Definition}
\label{subsec:portfolio}

A project \(i\in P\) is defined by its planned start \(s_i\), planned finish \(f_i\), planned duration \(D_i = f_i - s_i + 1\), and budget at completion \(\mathrm{BAC}_i\). The project is active only within its execution window \(\mathcal{A}_i = \{t : s_i \leq t \leq f_i\}\); the set of active projects at time \(t\) is \(P_t = \{i \in P : t \in \mathcal{A}_i\}\).

\subsection{Project State, Expenditure, and Cash Flows}
\label{subsec:state_cash}

The instantaneous condition of project \(i\) is summarised by a state vector
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{i,t} = \bigl( p_{i,t},\ \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\ \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t},\ RD_{i,t},\ h_{i,t},\ \rho_{i,t} \bigr),
\end{equation}
whose components are defined in \autoref{tab:state_vars}.

\subsubsection{Expenditure Dynamics}

Planned expenditure follows an S-curve modelled by the regularised incomplete Beta function:
\begin{equation}
F_i(\tau) = I_{\tau}(\alpha_i,\beta_i),\quad \tau = (t - s_i)/D_i \in [0,1],
\end{equation}
with \(\alpha_i,\beta_i>0\) controlling the front-loading of costs. Planned cumulative and period expenditures are \(\bar{E}_{i,t} = \mathrm{BAC}_i\,F_i(\tau_{i,t})\) and \(\bar{e}_{i,t} = \bar{E}_{i,t} - \bar{E}_{i,t-1}\). Actual expenditure \(e_{i,t}\) may deviate from the plan due to performance variations or management actions. The aggregate planned outflow is \(\bar{E}^{P}_{t} = \sum_{i\in P_t} \bar{e}_{i,t}\).

\subsubsection{Milestone-Based Revenue}

Each project generates a deterministic contract revenue \(R_i = \mathrm{BAC}_i(1+\pi_i)\) through a set of payment milestones \(K_i = \{0,1,\dots,N_i\}\). Milestone \(k\) has a payment fraction \(q_{i,k} \ge 0\) (\(\sum_k q_{i,k}=1\)) and a gross payment \(P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{gross}} = q_{i,k}R_i\).

Payment timing is progress-driven: a milestone \(k\) is planned at progress threshold \(\chi_{i,k}\) and is achieved at
\begin{equation}
t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{act}} = s_i + \frac{\chi_{i,k} D_i}{\overline{\mathrm{SPI}}_{i,k}},
\end{equation}
where \(\overline{\mathrm{SPI}}_{i,k}\) is an effective schedule performance measure. A client-side processing delay \(\delta_{i,k} \ge 0\) shifts the cash receipt to \(t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{rec}} = t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{act}} + \delta_{i,k}\) (advance payments, \(k=0\), are assumed to have \(\delta_{i,0}=0\)).

A retention rate \(r_i \in [0,1]\) is withheld from each payment. For non-final milestones, net receipt is \(P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{net}} = (1-r_i)P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{gross}}\). Accumulated retention is released at completion (milestone \(N_i\)):
\[
P_{i,N_i}^{\mathrm{net}} = (1-r_i)P_{i,N_i}^{\mathrm{gross}} + \sum_{j=0}^{N_i-1} r_i P_{i,j}^{\mathrm{gross}},
\]
so total lifecycle receipts satisfy \(\sum_k P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{net}} = R_i\).

Period revenue inflow from project \(i\) is \(Y_{i,t} = \sum_{k\in K_i} P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{net}} \,\mathbf{1}\{t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{rec}} = t\}\), and aggregate portfolio revenue is \(Y_t^P = \sum_{i\in P} Y_{i,t}\).

\subsubsection{Working Capital and Credit Limits}

Each project maintains a working capital account
\begin{equation}
W_{i,t} = W_{i,t-1} + Y_{i,t} - e_{i,t} - c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}},
\end{equation}
where \(c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}}\) are penalty costs from credit violations. A project-specific credit limit \(\mathrm{CL}_i\) is set, proportional to \(\mathrm{BAC}_i\), and may be reduced by advance payments:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CL}_i = \beta_i \, \mathrm{BAC}_i \, \bigl(1 - \gamma_i A_i \bigr),
\end{equation}
with \(A_i = q_{i,0}\) and parameters \(\beta_i, \gamma_i\) reflecting contractual and financial risk. A violation occurs when negative working capital exceeds the limit,
\[
V_{i,t} = \max\{0,\, -W_{i,t} - \mathrm{CL}_i\},
\]
and triggers a penalty cost \(c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}} = \rho_i V_{i,t}\) (or a more general convex function). Aggregate credit penalty costs are \(C_t^{\mathrm{credit}} = \sum_{i\in P_t} c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}}\).

\subsubsection{Funding Balance}

Total available budget in period \(t\) combines exogenous funds \(B^0_t\), revenue inflows, and external financing \(L_t\):
\[
B_t = B^0_t + Y_t^P + L_t.
\]
If required expenditure and intervention costs exceed \(B_t\), unmet funding \(U_t = \max\{0,\ \sum_{i\in P_t} e_{i,t} + C_t^{\mathrm{act}} - B_t\}\) arises, causing further delays or additional costs.

\subsection{Intervention Management}
\label{subsec:interventions}

Management can activate corrective interventions to recover schedule performance. Let \(u_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}\) indicate an intervention on project \(i\) at time \(t\). The cost of an intervention is proportional to the schedule gap \(G_{i,t} = \max\{0,\,1 - \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}\}\) and project size:
\begin{equation}
C_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}} = \kappa_i \, \mathrm{BAC}_i \, G_{i,t}, \quad \kappa_i \ge 0.
\end{equation}

During an intervention, schedule performance improves to \(\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t} + \eta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SPI}} (1 - \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t})\); a similar enhancement \(\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{eff}}\) may apply to cost performance. After the intervention ends, improvement decays smoothly towards a long‑run equilibrium:
\[
\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t^{\mathrm{end}}_i+s} = \mathrm{SPI}_{i}^{\mathrm{eq}} + (1-\rho_i) \Delta^{\mathrm{peak}}_{i} \exp(-\lambda_i^{\mathrm{decay}} s),
\]
with parameters governing peak improvement \(\Delta^{\mathrm{peak}}_{i}\), retention \(\rho_i\), and decay rate \(\lambda_i^{\mathrm{decay}}\).

The expected benefit of an intervention comprises delay avoidance, cost-overrun avoidance, and opportunity gains:
\begin{align}
B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{LD}}   &= \lambda_i \, \mathrm{BAC}_i \, \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[ \Delta D^{\mathrm{avoided}}_{i,t} \bigr], \label{eq:benefit_delay} \\
B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{cost}}  &= \mathrm{BAC}_i \, \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \frac{1}{\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}} - \frac{1}{\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{eff}}} \right], \\
B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{opp}}   &= r_i V_i \, \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[ \Delta D^{\mathrm{avoided}}_{i,t} \bigr], \notag
\end{align}
where \(\lambda_i\) is a delay penalty, \(V_i\) the project value rate, and \(\Delta D^{\mathrm{avoided}}_{i,t}\) the reduction in expected duration. Total expected benefit is \(\mathbb{E}[B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{tot}}] = B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{LD}} + B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{cost}} + B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{opp}}\).

Portfolio-level intervention expenditure \(C_t^{\mathrm{act}} = \sum_{i\in P_t} u_{i,t} C_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}}\) must respect an intervention budget \(B_t^{\mathrm{act}}\). A benefit–cost ratio \(\mathrm{BCR}_t^P\) provides a diagnostic when \(C_t^{\mathrm{act}} > 0\).

\subsection{Duration and Performance Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

Project duration is inherently uncertain. Baseline duration \(D_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}\) is drawn from a distribution \(\mathcal{D}_i\); realised duration can be expressed as \(D_i = D_i^{\mathrm{baseline}} M_i\), where \(M_i \ge 0\) captures disruptive or recovery effects. Performance indices \(\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}\) and \(\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}\) evolve stochastically, influenced by project state and external shocks. No specific parametric form is imposed; the formulation remains distributionally abstract.

\subsection{Sequential Decision Problem and Objective}
\label{subsec:decision_objective}

At each period \(t\), the decision maker observes the portfolio state \(\mathbf{\Omega}_t\), available budget \(B_t\), and intervention budget \(B_t^{\mathrm{act}}\), then chooses
\[
a_t = \bigl( \{e_{i,t}\}_{i\in P_t},\ \{u_{i,t}\}_{i\in P_t} \bigr),
\]
subject to the funding constraint \(\sum_{i\in P_t} e_{i,t} + C_t^{\mathrm{act}} \le B_t\), the intervention budget constraint \(\sum_{i\in P_t} u_{i,t} C_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}} \le B_t^{\mathrm{act}}\), and any project-specific operational limits.

The management goal is to find a policy \(\pi\) that maximises, in expectation, a composite portfolio performance measure reflecting the organisation's strategic posture. A generic representation that accommodates a spectrum from completion‑dominant to profit‑disciplined to capital‑preservation strategies is
\[
\max_{\pi}\; \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[ \sum_{t=1}^{H} \Bigl( \phi_V V_t - \phi_U U_t - \phi_D D_t - \phi_{\!C} C_t^{\mathrm{act}} - \phi_{\!Cr} C_t^{\mathrm{credit}} - \phi_{\!B} \sum_{i\in P_t} \frac{e_{i,t}}{\bar{e}} \,\mathbf{1}\{\hat{\mu}_{i,t} < \underline{\mu}_i\} \Bigr) \right],
\label{eq:objective}
\]
where
\begin{itemize}
\item \(V_t\) is realised project value or benefit in period \(t\),
\item \(U_t\) is unmet funding,
\item \(D_t\) is aggregate delay exposure,
\item \(C_t^{\mathrm{act}}\) and \(C_t^{\mathrm{credit}}\) are intervention and credit‑violation costs,
\item \(\hat{\mu}_{i,t}\) is an estimate of project \(i\)’s remaining profitability (e.g., expected continuation margin),
\item \(\underline{\mu}_i\) is a management‑defined threshold below which further allocation is deemed uneconomical,
\item and the non‑negative coefficients \(\phi_{(\cdot)}\) encode the relative importance of each component.
\end{itemize}

The final term explicitly penalises the allocation of budget to projects whose expected continuation value has fallen below a critical threshold, thereby inducing the rational suspension of economically non‑viable work. The exact choice of the performance components, the functional form of the penalty for poor‑prospect projects, and the relative weights \(\phi_{(\cdot)}\) are exogenous parameters set by management to reflect its risk appetite, financial strategy, and contractual environment. The formulation is therefore not tied to a single objective but provides a flexible template that can be calibrated to different organisational contexts.

\subsection{Assumptions, Scope, and Limitations}
\label{subsec:assumptions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The project portfolio is fixed; admission and selection decisions are outside the scope.
    \item Expenditure profiles are represented conceptually by S‑curves; exact shapes may vary by project.
    \item Duration, cost, and performance uncertainties are kept distributionally abstract.
    \item Intervention effects follow a smooth post‑intervention decay; in practice, performance may jump due to external shocks.
    \item Retention is released in full at project completion; a separate defects liability period is not modelled, as it extends beyond the active planning horizon.
    \item Credit limits are soft: violations incur penalty costs rather than forcing immediate project termination, and the organisation is assumed to be a rational, profit‑conscious contractor that may suspend projects whose expected net contribution becomes negative.
    \item The formulation is solution‑agnostic and can be addressed by various sequential decision‑making methods.
\end{itemize}
