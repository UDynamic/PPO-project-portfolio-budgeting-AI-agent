\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

\subsection{Management Problem and Portfolio Setting}
\label{subsec:setting}

We consider a finite-horizon, discrete-time budget management problem for a committed portfolio of projects under schedule, cost, and funding uncertainty. The decision maker does not select which projects to undertake; instead, the task is to allocate a constrained budget over periods $t = 1,\dots,H$ among an existing set of projects $P$, while monitoring progress and deciding when to activate corrective interventions. Each project consumes resources gradually, generates revenue through milestone payments, and may suffer delays or cost overruns. The objective is to minimize a composite loss function that penalises schedule delays, unmet funding, intervention costs, and credit violations.

A project $i\in P$ is defined by its planned start $s_i$, planned finish $f_i$, planned duration $D_i = f_i - s_i + 1$, and budget at completion $\mathrm{BAC}_i$. It is active only within its execution window $\mathcal{A}_i = \{t : s_i \leq t \leq f_i\}$, and the set of active projects at time $t$ is $P_t = \{i \in P : t \in \mathcal{A}_i\}$.

\subsection{Project State, Expenditure, and Cash Flows}
\label{subsec:state_cash}

The condition of project $i$ at time $t$ is fully captured by a state vector
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{i,t} = \bigl( p_{i,t},\ \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\ \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t},\ RD_{i,t},\ h_{i,t},\ \rho_{i,t} \bigr),
\end{equation}
where the meaning of each component is given in \autoref{tab:state_vars}.
\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Project state variables}
\label{tab:state_vars}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Description & Range \\
\midrule
$p_{i,t}$       & Physical progress (fraction completed) & $[0,1]$ \\
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$ & Schedule performance index (earned/planned) & $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ \\
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$ & Cost performance index (earned/actual) & $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ \\
$RD_{i,t}$      & Remaining planned duration (periods) & $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ \\
$h_{i,t}$       & Intervention-persistence state & $\{0,1\}$ or ordinal \\
$\rho_{i,t}$    & Replanning indicator & $\{0,1\}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Planned expenditure follows an S-curve modelled by the regularised incomplete Beta function:
\begin{equation}
F_i(\tau) = I_{\tau}(\alpha_i,\beta_i),\quad \tau = (t - s_i)/D_i \in [0,1],
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_i,\beta_i > 0$ controlling the front-loading of costs. The planned cumulative and period expenditures are $\bar{E}_{i,t} = \mathrm{BAC}_i\,F_i(\tau_{i,t})$ and $\bar{e}_{i,t} = \bar{E}_{i,t} - \bar{E}_{i,t-1}$.

Actual expenditure $e_{i,t}$ may deviate from the plan due to performance variations or management actions. The aggregate planned outflow is $\bar{E}^{P}_{t} = \sum_{i\in P_t} \bar{e}_{i,t}$.

\subsubsection{Milestone-Based Revenue}

Each project $i$ generates a deterministic contract revenue $R_i = \mathrm{BAC}_i(1+\pi_i)$ through a set of payment milestones $K_i = \{0,1,\dots,N_i\}$. Milestone $k$ has a payment fraction $q_{i,k} \ge 0$ ($\sum_k q_{i,k}=1$) and a gross payment $P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{gross}} = q_{i,k}R_i$.

Payment timing is progress-driven: a milestone $k$ is planned at progress threshold $\chi_{i,k}$ and is actually achieved at
\begin{equation}
t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{act}} = s_i + \frac{\chi_{i,k} D_i}{\overline{\mathrm{SPI}}_{i,k}},
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\mathrm{SPI}}_{i,k}$ is an effective schedule performance measure. A client-side processing delay $\delta_{i,k} \ge 0$ shifts the cash receipt to $t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{rec}} = t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{act}} + \delta_{i,k}$. Advance payments ($k=0$) are assumed to have $\delta_{i,0}=0$.

A retention rate $r_i \in [0,1]$ is withheld from each payment as security. For non-final milestones, net receipt is $P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{net}} = (1-r_i)P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{gross}}$. Accumulated retention is released at project completion (milestone $N_i$), so the final net payment includes all past withheld amounts:
\begin{equation}
P_{i,N_i}^{\mathrm{net}} = (1-r_i)P_{i,N_i}^{\mathrm{gross}} + \sum_{j=0}^{N_i-1} r_i P_{i,j}^{\mathrm{gross}}.
\end{equation}
Total lifecycle receipts satisfy $\sum_k P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{net}} = R_i$.

Period revenue inflow from project $i$ is
\begin{equation}
Y_{i,t} = \sum_{k\in K_i} P_{i,k}^{\mathrm{net}} \,\mathbf{1}\{t_{i,k}^{\mathrm{rec}} = t\},
\end{equation}
and aggregate portfolio revenue is $Y_t^P = \sum_{i\in P} Y_{i,t}$.

\subsubsection{Working Capital and Credit Limits}

Each project maintains a working capital account that evolves as
\begin{equation}
W_{i,t} = W_{i,t-1} + Y_{i,t} - e_{i,t} - c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}},
\end{equation}
where $c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}}$ represents penalty costs from credit violations. A project-specific credit limit $\mathrm{CL}_i$ is set, proportional to $\mathrm{BAC}_i$, and may be reduced by advance payments:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CL}_i = \beta_i \, \mathrm{BAC}_i \, \bigl(1 - \gamma_i A_i \bigr),
\end{equation}
with $A_i = q_{i,0}$ and parameters $\beta_i, \gamma_i$ reflecting contractual and financial risk. A violation occurs when negative working capital exceeds the limit,
\begin{equation}
V_{i,t} = \max\{0,\, -W_{i,t} - \mathrm{CL}_i\},
\end{equation}
and generates a penalty cost $c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}} = \rho_i V_{i,t}$ (or another convex function). The portfolio credit penalty cost is $C_t^{\mathrm{credit}} = \sum_{i\in P_t} c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{credit}}$.

\subsubsection{Funding Balance}

The total budget available in period $t$ combines exogenous funds $B^0_t$, revenue inflows, and any external financing $L_t$:
\begin{equation}
B_t = B^0_t + Y_t^P + L_t.
\end{equation}
If required expenditure and intervention costs exceed $B_t$, unmet funding $U_t = \max\{0,\ \sum_{i\in P_t} e_{i,t} + C_t^{\mathrm{act}} - B_t\}$ arises, triggering delays, re‑baselining, or additional costs.

\subsection{Intervention Management}
\label{subsec:interventions}

Management can activate corrective interventions to recover schedule performance. Let $u_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ indicate whether an intervention is applied to project $i$ at time $t$. The cost of activating an intervention is proportional to the schedule gap $G_{i,t} = \max\{0,\,1 - \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}\}$ and project size:
\begin{equation}
C_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}} = \kappa_i \, \mathrm{BAC}_i \, G_{i,t}, \quad \kappa_i \ge 0.
\end{equation}

During an intervention, the effective schedule performance improves to
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t} + \eta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{SPI}} (1 - \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}),
\end{equation}
and a similar enhancement $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t} (1 + \eta_{i,t}^{\mathrm{CPI}})$ may apply to cost performance. After the intervention ends, the improvement decays smoothly towards a long‑run equilibrium:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t^{\mathrm{end}}_i+s} = \mathrm{SPI}_{i}^{\mathrm{eq}} + (1-\rho_i) \Delta^{\mathrm{peak}}_{i} \exp(-\lambda_i^{\mathrm{decay}} s),
\end{equation}
with parameters governing peak improvement $\Delta^{\mathrm{peak}}_{i}$, retention $\rho_i$, and decay rate $\lambda_i^{\mathrm{decay}}$.

The expected benefit of an intervention is composed of delay avoidance, cost overrun avoidance, and opportunity gains:
\begin{align}
B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{LD}}   &= \lambda_i \, \mathrm{BAC}_i \, \mathbb{E}\bigl[ \Delta D^{\mathrm{avoided}}_{i,t} \bigr], \label{eq:benefit_delay} \\
B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{cost}}  &= \mathrm{BAC}_i \, \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \frac{1}{\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}} - \frac{1}{\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{eff}}} \right], \\
B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{opp}}   &= r_i V_i \, \mathbb{E}\bigl[ \Delta D^{\mathrm{avoided}}_{i,t} \bigr], \notag
\end{align}
with $\lambda_i$ a delay penalty, $V_i$ the project value rate, and $\Delta D^{\mathrm{avoided}}_{i,t}$ the reduction in expected duration. Total expected intervention benefit is $\mathbb{E}[B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{tot}}] = B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{LD}} + B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{cost}} + B_{i,t}^{\mathrm{opp}}$.

At the portfolio level, intervention expenditure $C_t^{\mathrm{act}} = \sum_{i\in P_t} u_{i,t} C_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}}$ must respect an intervention budget $B_t^{\mathrm{act}}$. A benefit–cost ratio $\mathrm{BCR}_t^P$ (computed when $C_t^{\mathrm{act}} > 0$) provides a diagnostic measure.

\subsection{Duration and Performance Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

Project duration is inherently uncertain. Baseline duration $D_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}$ is drawn from a distribution $\mathcal{D}_i$; the realised duration can be expressed as $D_i = D_i^{\mathrm{baseline}} M_i$, where $M_i \ge 0$ captures disruptive or recovery effects. Performance indices $\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$ and $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$ evolve stochastically, influenced by the project state and external shocks. No specific parametric form is imposed; the formulation remains distributionally abstract.

\subsection{Sequential Decision Problem and Objective}
\label{subsec:decision_objective}

At each period $t$, the decision maker observes the portfolio state $\mathbf{\Omega}_t = (\Omega_{i,t})_{i\in P_t}$, the available budget $B_t$, and the intervention budget $B_t^{\mathrm{act}}$, then chooses
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl( \{e_{i,t}\}_{i\in P_t},\ \{u_{i,t}\}_{i\in P_t} \bigr),
\end{equation}
subject to the funding constraint $\sum_{i\in P_t} e_{i,t} + C_t^{\mathrm{act}} \le B_t$, the intervention budget constraint $\sum_{i\in P_t} u_{i,t} C_{i,t}^{\mathrm{act}} \le B_t^{\mathrm{act}}$, and any project-specific operational limits. The chosen actions influence the evolution of project states, working capital, and future budgets.

The management objective is to find a policy $\pi$ that minimises the expected total management loss over the horizon:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\pi} \; \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[ \sum_{t=1}^{H} \Bigl( c_U U_t + c_D D_t + C_t^{\mathrm{act}} + C_t^{\mathrm{credit}} - V_t \Bigr) \right],
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $U_t$ is unmet funding, $D_t$ aggregate delay exposure, $C_t^{\mathrm{act}}$ total intervention cost, $C_t^{\mathrm{credit}}$ credit-violation costs, and $V_t$ realised project value or benefit. The positive coefficients $c_U, c_D$ reflect the relative weight of funding shortages and delays.

\subsection{Assumptions, Scope, and Limitations}
\label{subsec:assumptions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The project portfolio is fixed; admission and selection decisions are outside the scope.
    \item Expenditure profiles are represented conceptually by S‑curves; exact shapes may vary by project.
    \item Duration, cost, and performance uncertainties are kept distributionally abstract.
    \item Intervention effects follow a smooth post‑intervention decay; in reality, performance may jump due to external shocks.
    \item Retention is released in full at project completion; a separate defects liability period is not modelled, as it extends beyond the active planning horizon.
    \item Credit limits are soft: violations incur penalty costs rather than forcing project termination.
    \item The formulation is solution‑agnostic and can be addressed by various sequential decision‑making methods.
\end{itemize}
