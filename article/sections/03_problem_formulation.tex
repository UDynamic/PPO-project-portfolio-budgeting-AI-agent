The formulation proceeds across three levels of increasing generality,
all sharing the environment primitives defined in this section.
\textbf{Level~1} (Section~\ref{subsec:level1}) establishes the deterministic
full-foresight MILP whose optimum is a provable upper bound on any causal
policy. \textbf{Level~2} (Section~\ref{subsec:level2}) introduces stochastic
contractor efficiency and characterises the resulting multi-stage stochastic
program, establishing exponential scenario-tree growth. \textbf{Level~3}
(Section~\ref{subsec:level3}) reformulates the intractable stochastic program
as a Constrained Markov Decision Process, yielding the Gymnasium-compatible
environment on which the PPO agent is trained. The three levels form a coherent
arc --- Level~1 defines the performance ceiling by fixing both uncertain
primitives (budget-to-progress efficiency $\eta_{i,t}$ and milestone
payment timing delay $\lambda_{i,j}$) to known scenario values,
Level~2 explains why the resulting stochastic program is unreachable
by causal policies, Level~3 provides the tractable path.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Problem Statement and Scope}
\label{subsec:problem_statement}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The problem is operational, not strategic. A portfolio of $n$ contracted
projects is fixed: commitments are signed, start dates are scheduled, and
contracts are parameterised consistently with FIDIC Red, Yellow, and Silver
Book forms \citep{FIDIC2017}. The decision-maker --- a portfolio manager,
board, or programme director --- exercises a single recurring lever at each
discrete planning period $t \in \mathcal{T} = \{1,\ldots,H\}$: the budget 
$x_{i,t} \geq 0$ directed to each active project $i$.
The planning horizon $H$ is not a fixed configuration parameter but is
determined endogenously as $H = \max_i f_i- \min_i s_i + 1$, where $s_i$
and $f_i$ are the planned start and finish of project $i$; the index is
shifted so that period $t=1$ aligns with the earliest project start.
All outcomes of interest --- milestone achievement, cash inflows, schedule adherence, and
project termination --- flow through this allocation decision. Portfolio
selection is treated as an exogenous upstream input \citep{Archer1999}.

Four structural assumptions underlie the entire formulation.
(i)~\emph{Independent bilateral contracts}: projects interact only through
the shared cash balance; no resource or workforce sharing occurs, consistent
with standard EPC contracting practice.
(ii)~\emph{Budget as the sole control}: the contractor's internal resource
decisions are absorbed into a stochastic efficiency parameter $\eta_{i,t}$;
the portfolio manager observes how much physical progress a budget allocation
yields, not how it is achieved. A second independent stochastic primitive,
the milestone payment timing delay $\lambda_{i,j} \in \{0,1,\ldots,d_i^{\max}\}$,
governs when the contractually fixed net payment $R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}$ is
actually received after milestone $j$ is certified. Both $\eta_{i,t}$ and
$\lambda_{i,j}$ are unobservable at the time the relevant decision must be
committed: $\eta_{i,t}$ is unknown when $x_{i,t}$ is committed;
$\lambda_{i,j}$ is unknown at the period of certification. The timing of that allocation --- specifically
how quickly milestones are certified --- determines the discounted value of
inflows under $\gamma < 1$, making the scheduling of spend, not just its
total magnitude, the operative decision variable for a return-maximising
portfolio manager \citep{Damodaran2012}.
(iii)~\emph{Discrete monthly periods}: $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\ldots,H\}$,
where $H$ is determined dynamically from the portfolio
(see Section~\ref{subsubsec:sets}), reflects the standard monthly
governance cycle of portfolio management
(iv)~\emph{FIDIC contract mechanics}: milestone payments, advance payment
and recovery, retention, and termination settlement are all calibrated
parameters, not fixed constants, accommodating the full range of particular
conditions encountered in practice.

\subsubsection{Sets, Indices, and the Decision Variable}
\label{subsubsec:sets}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6cm, labelwidth=2.4cm]
  \item[$\mathcal{P}$]
    Projects $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, indexed by $i$; all committed at horizon
    start.
  \item[$\mathcal{T}$]
    Planning periods $\{1,\ldots,H\}$, indexed by $t$ (calendar months),
    where $H = \max_{i\in\mathcal{P}} f_i- \min_{i\in\mathcal{P}} s_i + 1$
    is derived endogenously from the committed portfolio; period $t=1$
    corresponds to the calendar month of the earliest project start
    $\min_i s_i$.
  \item[$\mathcal{M}_i$]
    Ordered milestones $\{1,\ldots,M_i\}$ for project $i$, indexed by $j$,
    with progress thresholds $\theta_{i,1} < \cdots < \theta_{i,M_i} = 1$.
  \item[$\mathcal{A}_i^{\mathrm{plan}}$]
    Planned execution window $\{s_i,\ldots,f_i\}$; anchors S-curve
    computations.
  \item[$\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$]
    Projects active at period $t$; fixed at Level~1, endogenous at
    Levels~2--3.
  \item[$\mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t)$]
    Milestones of project $i$ certified by period $t$:
    $\{j : \theta_{i,j} \leq P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    \text{ and } t \geq e_{i,j}\}$,
    where $e_{i,j} \in \mathcal{T}$ is the contractually specified
    earliest certification period for milestone $j$.
  \item[$x_{i,t} \geq 0$]
    \textbf{Decision variable}: budget allocated to project $i$ at period
    $t$. The period-$t$ action vector is
    $\mathbf{x}_t =
    (x_{i,t})_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}$.
  \item[$B_t \geq 0$]
    Portfolio cash balance at the start of period $t$; $B_1$ is given.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Progress and Expenditure Dynamics}
\label{subsubsec:progress}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Budget $x_{i,t}$ converts to physical progress through the efficiency
scalar $\eta_{i,t}$, which absorbs all sources of conversion uncertainty
(contractor productivity, supply chain, weather):
\begin{equation}
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    = P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}
      + \eta_{i,t} \cdot \frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t} = \mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t-1} + x_{i,t},
  \label{eq:progress_rec}
\end{equation}
with $P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}} = \mathrm{ACWP}_{i,0} = 0$. Here
$\mathrm{BAC}_i$ is the Budget at Completion --- the total planned
contractor expenditure for project $i$, which serves as the EVM cost
basis and the normalisation denominator for all progress and performance
calculations. At Level~1, $\eta_{i,t}$ is replaced by a known
deterministic value $\bar{\eta}_{i,t}$; at Levels~2--3 it is a
stochastic primitive realised only after $x_{i,t}$ is committed.

The planned cumulative expenditure fraction follows a normalised S-curve
\citep{Kenley2009, barraza2004probabilistic} parameterised by shape
parameters $(a_i, b_i)$:
\begin{equation}
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}
    = I_{\tau_{i,t}^c}(a_i, b_i),
  \qquad
  \tau_{i,t} = \frac{t - s_i + 1}{D_i^{\mathrm{plan}}},
  \quad
  \tau_{i,t}^c = \min\{1,\max\{0,\tau_{i,t}\}\},
  \label{eq:scurve}
\end{equation}
where $I_\tau(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the regularized incomplete Beta function
and $D_i^{\mathrm{plan}} = f_i - s_i + 1$ is the planned duration. This
profile is a fixed parameter derived at commitment; it is never a decision
variable.

\subsubsection{Contractual Cash-Flow Mechanics}
\label{subsubsec:cashflow}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Payment inflows follow FIDIC mechanics \citep{FIDIC2017}. At project
initiation ($t = s_i$), the portfolio manager receives the advance payment
$A_i = \alpha_i \cdot CP_i$, where $\alpha_i$ is the advance ratio and
$CP_i = \mathrm{BAC}_i(1+\pi_i)$ is the contract price with profit margin
$\pi_i$. The contract price is the sole payment accumulation baseline:
all milestone revenues, advance payment, retention, and final settlement
sum to $CP_i$. All EVM performance signals and progress calculations are
normalised against $\mathrm{BAC}_i$.

Milestone $j$ is certified at the first period $t$ at which
$P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \geq \theta_{i,j}$ \emph{and}
$t \geq e_{i,j}$, where $e_{i,j}$ is the contractually specified
earliest certification period. Under FIDIC Sub-Clause~14.3, interim
payment applications may not be submitted before the intervals stated
in the Appendix to Tender \citep{FIDIC2017}; $e_{i,j}$ parameterises
this constraint directly, preventing a portfolio manager from
accelerating cash inflows beyond the contractual payment schedule
regardless of how quickly physical progress is achieved. The net payment received,
after retention withholding at rate $\rho_i$ and advance recovery
deduction at rate $\delta_i^{\mathrm{rec}}$ (commencing once cumulative
certified payments exceed threshold $\psi_i$), is:
\begin{equation}
  R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}
    = \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i (1 - \rho_i)
      - \delta_i^{\mathrm{rec}} \cdot \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i
        \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\left\{
          \textstyle\sum_{k \leq j} \phi_{i,k} \geq \psi_i
        \right\},
  \label{eq:Rnet}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{i,j}$ is the contractual weight of milestone $j$ with
$\sum_j \phi_{i,j} = 1$. This parameterisation is consistent with FIDIC
Sub-Clause~14.2, under which advance recovery deductions commence once
cumulative certified interim payments exceed a specified fraction of the
contract price \citep{FIDIC2017}. Upon project completion
($P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 1$), the accumulated retention
$R_i^{\mathrm{ret}} = \rho_i \cdot CP_i$ is released together with the
final milestone payment. The payment identity
$A_i + \sum_j R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}} + R_i^{\mathrm{ret}} = CP_i$
holds exactly.

Milestone payments are subject to stochastic timing delays between
certification and receipt at Levels~2--3 \citep{Aibinu2006, Odeh2002}.
The amount is fixed at the contractual net value $R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}$,
but the period of receipt is determined by a random delay
$\lambda_{i,j} \in \{0,1,\ldots,d_i^{\max}\}$ drawn at certification:
payment for milestone $j$ certified at period $\tau_{i,j}^{\mathrm{cert}}$
is received at period $\tau_{i,j}^{\mathrm{cert}} + \lambda_{i,j}$.
At Level~1, $\lambda_{i,j}$ is replaced by a known deterministic value
$\bar{\lambda}_{i,j}$; at Levels~2--3 it is a stochastic primitive
realised only after the milestone is certified.
Aggregate period inflow is:
\begin{equation}
  V_t
    = \sum_{i,j} R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}
        \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\left\{
          \tau_{i,j}^{\mathrm{cert}} + \lambda_{i,j} = t
        \right\}
      + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{term}}} R_i^{\mathrm{term}},
  \label{eq:Vt}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{term}}$ collects projects newly terminated
at $t$, with settlement $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ defined in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination} below.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Performance Monitoring: EVM Signals}
\label{subsubsec:evm}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The Earned Value Management framework \citep{Fleming2010} provides the
diagnostic vocabulary used to monitor contractor performance and to drive
the termination mechanic. The Earned Value at period $t$ is
$\mathrm{EV}_{i,t} = P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i$
(equivalently, the Budgeted Cost of Work Performed, BCWP), expressed in
cost units against the $\mathrm{BAC}_i$ baseline. Three derived indices
are computed each period:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}
    &= \frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}}{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{plan}}},
  \label{eq:SPI} \\
  \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}
    &= \frac{\mathrm{EV}_{i,t}}{\mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t}}
     = \frac{P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i}
            {\mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t}},
  \label{eq:CPI} \\
  \mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}
    &= \mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t}
       + \frac{\mathrm{BAC}_i - \mathrm{EV}_{i,t}}{\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}}.
  \label{eq:EAC}
\end{align}
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t} < 1$ signals schedule lag;
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t} < 1$ signals cost overrun.
$\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$ is the forward-looking Estimate at Completion: it
projects the total cost of the project at the current cost performance
rate by scaling remaining planned work by the inverse of
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$, directly analogous to the forecast finish date
computation used in Condition~1 of the termination mechanic
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:termination}). \citet{christensen1993est} established
that this formulation produces the most accurate and stable
cost-at-completion forecasts, with reliability increasing beyond the 20\%
completion mark \citep{Lipke2003}. All three indices are observable state
variables; their stochastic dynamics at Levels~2--3 are calibrated from
empirical EVM datasets \citep{Acebes2015, Batselier2017}.

\subsubsection{Termination Mechanic}
\label{subsubsec:termination}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Termination is an endogenous environment mechanic, not an agent action.
At each period $t$, the environment evaluates two conditions for every
active project $i$. Both are forward-looking trajectory estimates,
symmetric in structure to one another.

\paragraph{Condition 1 --- Schedule tolerance exhausted.}
The forecast finish date, computed by linear extrapolation of remaining
planned duration at the current schedule performance rate, is:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{f}_{i,t}
    = t + \frac{D_i^{\mathrm{plan}} - (t - s_i)}{\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}}.
  \label{eq:fhat}
\end{equation}
The schedule deviation is $\Delta f_{i,t} = \hat{f}_{i,t} - f_i$.
Condition~1 activates when the projected delay exceeds the contractual
schedule tolerance $\Omega_i$ (expressed in periods):
\begin{equation}
  \Delta f_{i,t} \;>\; \Omega_i.
  \label{eq:term_cond1}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Condition 2 --- Cost overrun trajectory unacceptable.}

% ⚠️ adding the $\delta{AC_{i,t}}$ as the marker for this distance. use similar namings such as the previous conditions

The Estimate at Completion $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$ defined in
\eqref{eq:EAC} projects the total project cost at the current cost
performance rate, by scaling remaining planned work by the inverse of
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$ --- directly analogous to the schedule extrapolation
in \eqref{eq:fhat}. Condition~2 activates when the projected total cost
exceeds the project-specific maximum tolerable cost overrun:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{EAC}_{i,t} \;>\; (1 + \mu_i) \cdot \mathrm{BAC}_i,
  \label{eq:term_cond2}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_i \geq 0$ is the contractually set maximum cost overrun ratio
for project $i$, fixed at commitment.

\paragraph{Cure-period counter and termination execution.}
When both conditions hold simultaneously, the environment decrements the
project-specific cure-period counter, modelling the FIDIC
Sub-Clause~15.1 obligation to allow a recovery interval before
termination may be executed \citep{FIDIC2017, Ndekugri2008}:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}
    = \begin{cases}
        \tau_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{rem}} - 1
          & \text{if \eqref{eq:term_cond1} and \eqref{eq:term_cond2}
                  both hold,} \\
        \tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}}
          & \text{if either condition lapses.}
      \end{cases}
  \label{eq:counter}
\end{equation}
The counter initialises at
$\tau_{i,s_i}^{\mathrm{rem}} = \tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}}$, where
$\tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}} \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ is the project-specific
tolerance, fixed at commitment. Termination executes at the period
immediately following
$t_i^{\mathrm{term}} = \min\{t : \tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}} = 0\}$.
The counter is part of the observable portfolio state; the agent cannot
override its dynamics but influences both conditions indirectly through
allocation decisions that drive $\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$ and
$\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$.

Upon termination at $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$, the net settlement payment
is:
\begin{equation}
  R_i^{\mathrm{term}}
    = P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}} \cdot CP_i (1-\rho_i)
      - \Bigl(A_i
          - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{M}_i^{\mathrm{cert}}(t_i^{\mathrm{term}})}
              \delta_i^{\mathrm{rec}} \cdot \phi_{i,j} \cdot CP_i
              \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\Bigl\{
                \textstyle\sum_{k \leq j} \phi_{i,k} \geq \psi_i
              \Bigr\}
        \Bigr).
  \label{eq:term_settlement}
\end{equation}
The first term is the value of work delivered net of retention; the
second is the outstanding advance not yet recovered through milestone
deductions. Negative $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ constitutes a net contractor obligation,
recovered from portfolio inflows in subsequent periods consistent with
FIDIC termination clauses \citep{FIDIC2017}. The settlement formula
values delivered work at $P_{i,t_i^{\mathrm{term}}}^{\mathrm{actual}}
\cdot CP_i(1-\rho_i)$ --- the fraction of contract price attributable
to physically completed scope, net of retention. This is not a penalty
construct; it is a direct valuation of productive assets transferred at
termination \citep{Dixit1994}, grounding the model's financial signals
in the real asset value of work in place rather than in arbitrarily
weighted deviation terms. A portfolio manager interpreting a negative
$R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ correctly reads it as the cost of having disbursed
an advance against work that was never delivered --- a capital allocation
error whose financial consequence is fully determined by the contract
without requiring external calibration.

\subsubsection{Portfolio Cash Balance}
\label{subsubsec:balance}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The portfolio cash balance evolves as:
\begin{equation}
  B_{t+1}
    = B_t
      - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
      + V_t,
  \qquad B_t \geq 0 \quad \forall\,t \in \mathcal{T}.
  \label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
The constraint $B_t \geq 0$ prohibits borrowing; the portfolio operates
under a fixed capital commitment \citep{kavadias2003resource}. The shared
balance $B_t$ is the sole coupling across otherwise independent project
dynamics.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Observable State and Shared Objective}
\label{subsubsec:objective}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The portfolio state at period $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
  S_t = \Bigl(
    \bigl\{
      P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}},\;
      \mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{SPI}_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{CPI}_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{EAC}_{i,t},\;
      \Delta f_{i,t},\;
      \mathrm{status}_{i,t},\;
      \tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}
    \bigr\}_{i \in \mathcal{P}},\;
    B_t
  \Bigr),
  \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{status}_{i,t} \in \{\texttt{active},\,
\texttt{completed},\, \texttt{terminated}\}$. This gives a state
dimension of $8n + 1$ per period. All components are observable;
$\eta_{i,t}$ is the sole unobservable primitive, realised only after
$x_{i,t}$ is committed.

The decision-maker maximises discounted net cash flow over the horizon:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\{x_{i,t}\}} \quad
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}
    \!\left(
      V_t
      - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
    \right)
  \right],
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
subject to, for all $i \in \mathcal{P}$, $t \in \mathcal{T}$:
\begin{align}
  x_{i,t} &\geq 0,
  \label{eq:nonnegativity} \\
  \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t} &\leq B_t,
  \label{eq:budget} \\
  x_{i,t} &= 0
    \quad \text{if } \mathrm{status}_{i,t} \neq \texttt{active}.
  \label{eq:inactive}
\end{align}


There is no terminal sum and no deviation penalty in the objective.
Completion incentives are embedded structurally: $R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}$
and $R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}$ enter $V_t$ at the final certification
period; $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ (possibly negative) enters at termination,
creating a natural deterrent against allowing project performance to
deteriorate to the termination threshold \citep{Ng1999}. Symmetrically,
the discounting of inflows by $\gamma^{t-1}$ in \eqref{eq:objective}
creates a completion incentive from above --- earlier certification of
milestone $j$ yields $R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}$ at a higher discount
weight, so a return-maximising agent will front-load allocations to
projects where earlier certification is feasible, without any additional
objective term. In competitive project-based markets where $\pi_i$ is
fixed at contract signing, this discounting effect is the formal
expression of the capital velocity argument: the annualised return on
committed capital increases with completion speed, and $\gamma$ encodes
the opportunity cost of capital that makes this concrete
\citep{Damodaran2012}.The expectation
$\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ collapses to a deterministic evaluation at Level~1
and is retained at Levels~2 and~3.

\subsection{Level~1: Deterministic Full-Foresight MILP}
\label{subsec:level1}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

Setting $\eta_{i,t} = \bar{\eta}_{i,t}$ (known) renders $S_t$ a
deterministic function of $\mathbf{x}_t$, so
$\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$, milestone certification times, and
termination periods $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ are all computable ex ante.
Under deterministic efficiency, the trajectories of
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$,
and the counter $\tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}}$ are fully determined before
solving; both termination conditions
\eqref{eq:term_cond1}--\eqref{eq:term_cond2} and the counter dynamics
\eqref{eq:counter} are parameter-verifiable, so termination periods
$t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ and their settlements $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ enter
the MILP as fixed parameters rather than optimisation constraints.

Introducing the certification binary $u_{i,j,t} \in \{0,1\}$ ($=1$ iff
milestone $j$ of project $i$ certified by period $t$) and activity
indicator $\mathrm{act}_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ (fixed parameter), the
stochastic objective~\eqref{eq:objective} collapses to:

\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:L1}
\begin{align}
  \max \quad
  & \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}\!\left(
      V_t^{\mathrm{det}}
      - \sum_{i} \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\, x_{i,t}
    \right)
  \label{eq:L1_obj} \\[4pt]
  \text{s.t.} \quad
  B_{t+1}
    &= B_t - \sum_i \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\,x_{i,t} + V_t^{\mathrm{det}},
    \quad B_t \geq 0,
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L1_balance} \\
  \sum_i \mathrm{act}_{i,t}\, x_{i,t}
    &\leq B_t,
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L1_budget} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &= P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}
       + \bar{\eta}_{i,t}\,\tfrac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
    \quad P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \leq 1,
    && \forall\, i,t
  \label{eq:L1_progress} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &\geq \theta_{i,j}\,u_{i,j,t}
       - \mathbf{M}(1 - u_{i,j,t}),
    && \forall\, i,j,t
  \label{eq:L1_cert_lb} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &\leq \theta_{i,j} + \mathbf{M}\,u_{i,j,t},
    \quad u_{i,j,t} \geq u_{i,j,t-1},
    \quad u_{i,j+1,t} \leq u_{i,j,t},
    && \forall\, i,j,t
  \label{eq:L1_cert_logic} \\
  u_{i,j,t}
    &= 0
    \quad \text{if } t < e_{i,j},
    && \forall\, i,j,t
  \label{eq:L1_earliest} \\
  V_t^{\mathrm{det}}
    &= \sum_{i,j} R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}\,(u_{i,j,t} - u_{i,j,t-1})
       + \sum_i R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}\,
           (u_{i,M_i,t} - u_{i,M_i,t-1})
       \notag \\
    &\quad
       + \sum_i A_i \cdot \mathbf{1}\{t = s_i\}
       + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{term}}}
           R_i^{\mathrm{term}}
           \cdot \mathbf{1}\{t = t_i^{\mathrm{term}}\},
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L1_cashflow} \\
  x_{i,t}
    &= 0 \quad \text{if } \mathrm{act}_{i,t} = 0,
    && \forall\, i,t
  \label{eq:L1_inactive} \\
  x_{i,t} &\geq 0,
    \quad u_{i,j,t} \in \{0,1\},
    \quad u_{i,j,0} = P_{i,0}^{\mathrm{actual}} = 0.
  \label{eq:L1_domains}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

The big-$M$ constant is $\mathbf{M} = \max_{i,j}\theta_{i,j}$.
Constraints~\eqref{eq:L1_cert_lb}--\eqref{eq:L1_cert_logic} implement
exact if-and-only-if certification logic with monotone, ordered binary
activation. Constraint~\eqref{eq:L1_earliest} enforces the contractual
earliest certification date: $u_{i,j,t}$ is fixed to zero for all
periods prior to $e_{i,j}$, preventing the MILP from certifying a
milestone before its contractual window opens regardless of how much
budget has been allocated. This makes the optimal allocation timing
uniquely determined under $\gamma < 1$: the agent will front-load
spend to reach $\theta_{i,j}$ exactly at $e_{i,j}$, then hold until
the window opens. The difference $(u_{i,j,t} - u_{i,j,t-1})$ in
\eqref{eq:L1_cashflow} equals one in the sole period a milestone is
first certified, linearising the payment trigger without additional
binaries.

\paragraph{Upper bound.}
Let $Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$ denote the Level~1 optimum. For any causal
policy $\pi$ feasible under uncertainty, $\sup_\pi \mathbb{E}[Z^\pi]
\leq Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$: every feasible causal allocation sequence is
also feasible in the Level~1 program solved on the realised trajectory,
so the full-foresight optimum dominates in expectation. The gap
$Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}} - Z^\pi$ quantifies the cost of uncertainty and
anchors the empirical comparison in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Level~2: Stochastic Extension and Intractability}
\label{subsec:level2}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Stochastic Primitive and Distributional Assumption}
\label{subsubsec:l2_primitive}

At Level~2, the deterministic efficiency scalar $\bar{\eta}_{i,t}$ is
replaced by a random variable $\eta_{i,t}$ defined on a probability
space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$. The efficiency realisations
are assumed to satisfy:
\begin{assumption}[Efficiency structure]
  \label{ass:eta}
  For each project $i \in \mathcal{P}$ and period $t \in \mathcal{T}$:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $\eta_{i,t} > 0$ almost surely;
    \item $\{\eta_{i,t}\}_{t \in \mathcal{T}}$ are independent across
      periods given project $i$;
    \item $\eta_{i,t}$ are independent across projects $i \in \mathcal{P}$;
    \item the marginal distribution of $\eta_{i,t}$ is project-specific
      and calibrated from empirical EVM datasets
      \citep{Acebes2015, Batselier2017}.
  \end{enumerate}
  \end{assumption}
  
  \begin{assumption}[Payment delay structure]
  \label{ass:lambda}
  For each project $i \in \mathcal{P}$ and milestone $j \in \mathcal{M}_i$:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $\lambda_{i,j} \in \{0,1,\ldots,d_i^{\max}\}$ with $d_i^{\max} \geq 0$;
    \item $\lambda_{i,j}$ are independent across milestones $j$ and projects $i$;
    \item $\lambda_{i,j}$ is independent of $\{\eta_{i,t}\}_{i,t}$;
    \item the distribution of $\lambda_{i,j}$ is project-specific and
      calibrated from empirical payment practice studies
      \citep{Aibinu2006, Assaf1995, Odeh2002}, with support
      $\{0,1,2,3\}$ as the default consistent with the literature.
  \end{enumerate}
  \end{assumption}
  
  The realisation $\eta_{i,t}(\omega)$ is revealed only after allocation
  $x_{i,t}$ is committed; the realisation $\lambda_{i,j}(\omega)$ is
  revealed only after milestone $j$ is certified. These two timing
  constraints define the two distinct decision points at which the portfolio
  manager faces irreducible uncertainty: the allocation decision under unknown
  productivity, and the cash-flow realisation under unknown payment timing.

\subsubsection{Information Structure and Non-Anticipativity}
\label{subsubsec:l2_filtration}

Let $\mathcal{F}_t$ denote the $\sigma$-algebra generated by all
efficiency realisations, all payment delay realisations for milestones
certified by period $t$, and the resulting state transitions, up to and
including period $t$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}_t = \sigma\!\bigl(
    \eta_{i,\tau}(\omega) : i \in \mathcal{P},\; \tau \leq t
  \bigr)
  \;\vee\;
  \sigma\!\bigl(
    \lambda_{i,j}(\omega) : i \in \mathcal{P},\;
    j \in \mathcal{M}_i,\;
    \tau_{i,j}^{\mathrm{cert}} \leq t
  \bigr),
  \qquad \mathcal{F}_0 = \{\emptyset, \Omega\}.
  \label{eq:filtration}
\end{equation}
The history available to the portfolio manager at the start of period
$t$, prior to committing $\mathbf{x}_t$, is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{H}_t = \bigl(S_1,\, \mathbf{x}_1,\,
                         S_2,\, \mathbf{x}_2,\, \ldots,\,
                         S_{t-1},\, \mathbf{x}_{t-1},\, S_t\bigr),
  \label{eq:history}
\end{equation}
where $S_t$ is the portfolio state defined in \eqref{eq:state}, which
is $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable since it reflects only realisations
up to period $t-1$. The \emph{non-anticipativity constraint} requires
that the allocation at period $t$ depends only on the available history:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_t \in \sigma(\mathcal{H}_t)
  \qquad \forall\, t \in \mathcal{T}.
  \label{eq:nonanticipativity}
\end{equation}
This constraint distinguishes a causal policy from the full-foresight
MILP of Level~1, which optimises over the entire realised trajectory
$(\eta_{i,t})_{i,t}$ simultaneously.

\subsubsection{The Multi-Stage Stochastic Program}
\label{subsubsec:l2_mssp}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:eta} and constraint \eqref{eq:nonanticipativity},
the Level~1 MILP \eqref{eq:L1} becomes a multi-stage stochastic program
\citep{Birge2011}. The decision at each stage is a \emph{policy}
$\pi_t : \mathcal{H}_t \to \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}|}_{\geq 0}$
rather than a fixed scalar, and the objective is an expectation over
all scenario realisations. The program is:
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:L2}
\begin{align}
  \max_{\{\pi_t\}} \quad
  & \mathbb{E}\!\left[
      \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}\!\left(
        V_t - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
      \right)
    \right]
  \label{eq:L2_obj} \\[4pt]
  \text{s.t.} \quad
  x_{i,t} &= \pi_t(\mathcal{H}_t)_i,
  \qquad \mathbf{x}_t \in \sigma(\mathcal{H}_t),
  && \forall\, i,\, t
  \label{eq:L2_nonant} \\
  P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}}
    &= P_{i,t-1}^{\mathrm{actual}}
       + \eta_{i,t} \cdot \frac{x_{i,t}}{\mathrm{BAC}_i},
    && \forall\, i,\, t
  \label{eq:L2_progress} \\
  B_{t+1}
    &= B_t
       - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
       + V_t,
    \quad B_t \geq 0,
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L2_balance} \\
  \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
    &\leq B_t,
    && \forall\, t
  \label{eq:L2_budget} \\
  x_{i,t} &= 0
    \quad \text{if } \mathrm{status}_{i,t} \neq \texttt{active},
    && \forall\, i,\, t
  \label{eq:L2_inactive} \\
  x_{i,t} &\geq 0,
    && \forall\, i,\, t
  \label{eq:L2_nonneg}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $V_t$ is as defined in \eqref{eq:Vt}, now a random variable
whose realisation depends on $(\eta_{i,\tau})_{\tau \leq t}$ through
the progress recursion \eqref{eq:L2_progress} and the milestone
certification events it generates. The termination counter
\eqref{eq:counter}, and therefore $\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$
and $\mathrm{status}_{i,t}$, are likewise random variables endogenous
to the realised efficiency trajectory, in contrast to Level~1 where
they were fixed parameters.

\subsubsection{Scenario-Tree Representation and Exponential Growth}
\label{subsubsec:l2_tree}

The canonical solution method for multi-stage stochastic programs is
scenario-tree enumeration \citep{Birge2011}. The scenario tree for
\eqref{eq:L2} has the following structure. Discretise $\eta_{i,t}$ to
$K$ values per project per period. At each period $t$, the tree branches
by $K^n$ (one branching factor of $K$ per project, with $n$ projects
independent by Assumption~\ref{ass:eta}(iii)). Over a horizon of $H$
periods the tree has:
\begin{equation}
  |\mathcal{S}| = K^{nH}
  \label{eq:tree_size}
\end{equation}
leaf nodes, each corresponding to a complete realisation of
$(\eta_{i,t})_{i \in \mathcal{P},\, t \in \mathcal{T}}$.

For a representative infrastructure portfolio with $n = 10$ projects,
a horizon $H$ derived endogenously from the committed portfolio
(typically $H \in [18, 36]$ months in practice), and $K = 5$ discrete
efficiency scenarios per project per period, the leaf-node count is:
\begin{equation}
  |\mathcal{S}| = 5^{10 \times 24} = 5^{240}
    \approx 10^{167},
  \label{eq:tree_example}
\end{equation}
a number that exceeds the estimated number of atoms in the observable
universe by more than 80 orders of magnitude. This is not a
consequence of the binary milestone variables inherited from the MILP
structure: replacing them with continuous relaxations leaves the
exponent $nH$ unchanged, since the exponential growth is intrinsic to
the multi-stage stochastic formulation and independent of the
within-stage problem structure.

\paragraph{Endogenous horizon compounding.}
A further compounding factor distinguishes this problem from standard
multi-stage stochastic programs with fixed horizons. The planning
horizon $H = \max_i f_i - \min_i s_i + 1$ is itself a function of the
committed portfolio; it is not an external configuration parameter.
Under stochastic efficiency, the effective execution window of each
project is uncertain — a project with $\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t} < 1$
throughout will overrun $f_i$, extending the realised horizon beyond
$H$. In the worst case, every project overruns simultaneously, and the
actual number of periods over which decisions must be made grows with
the portfolio. Each additional period multiplies the scenario tree by
$K^n$, so horizon uncertainty compounds \eqref{eq:tree_size}
multiplicatively.

\paragraph{Termination as structural bifurcation.}
At Level~1, termination periods $t_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ were fixed
parameters precomputed from the known efficiency trajectory. At
Level~2, the period at which the cure-period counter
\eqref{eq:counter} reaches zero depends on the realised sequence of
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$ and $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$ values, which are in
turn determined by $(\eta_{i,\tau})_{\tau \leq t}$. Each termination
event removes project $i$ from $\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$ and
delivers $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ into $V_t$ — a structural change in the
active project set that bifurcates the scenario tree at every period
in which a project's counter is positive. Across $n$ projects each
with counter depth $\tau_i^{\mathrm{tol}}$, the number of
structurally distinct subtrees grows combinatorially in the
termination event space, adding a second source of exponential scaling
beyond \eqref{eq:tree_size}.

\subsubsection{Intractability and the Motivation for Level~3}
\label{subsubsec:l2_intractability}

The exponential scaling established in \eqref{eq:tree_size} constitutes
a fundamental computational barrier. It is not a function of solver
technology or implementation efficiency; it is intrinsic to the
multi-stage stochastic formulation of any sequential decision problem
with independent per-stage uncertainty across $n$ components over $H$
stages \citep{Birge2011}. Approximation methods for multi-stage
stochastic programs --- Sample Average Approximation \citep{Kleywegt2002},
Progressive Hedging \citep{Rockafellar1991}, and Stochastic Dual
Dynamic Programming \citep{Pereira1991} --- reduce the computational
burden but do not escape the exponential scaling in $n$ and $H$ at
portfolio scales of practical interest. Sample Average Approximation,
for instance, requires a sample size that grows with the variance of
the objective and the number of stages to achieve a given optimality
gap \citep{Kleywegt2002}; for $n = 10$ and $H = 24$ this remains
intractable without structural decomposition that the shared cash
balance \eqref{eq:L2_balance} prevents.

\begin{proposition}[Level~1 as upper bound]
\label{prop:upper_bound}
Let $Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$ denote the optimal value of the Level~1 MILP
\eqref{eq:L1} evaluated on a realised efficiency trajectory
$(\bar{\eta}_{i,t})_{i,t}$. Let $Z^\pi$ denote the objective value
achieved by any causal policy $\pi$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:nonanticipativity} on the same trajectory. Then:
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{\pi \in \Pi^{\mathrm{causal}}}
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[Z^\pi\right]
  \leq Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}},
  \label{eq:upper_bound}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi^{\mathrm{causal}}$ denotes the set of all policies
satisfying \eqref{eq:nonanticipativity}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix any realised trajectory $\omega \in \Omega$. Any allocation
sequence $\{x_{i,t}\}$ produced by a causal policy $\pi$ under
$\omega$ is a feasible solution to the Level~1 MILP solved on
$\omega$ (since feasibility constraints \eqref{eq:L1_balance}--
\eqref{eq:L1_inactive} are identical to
\eqref{eq:L2_balance}--\eqref{eq:L2_inactive} for fixed
$\bar{\eta}_{i,t} = \eta_{i,t}(\omega)$). Therefore
$Z^\pi(\omega) \leq Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}(\omega)$ for every $\omega$.
Taking expectations on both sides yields \eqref{eq:upper_bound}.
\end{proof}

The gap $Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}} - \mathbb{E}[Z^\pi]$ quantifies the cost
of uncertainty: the value destroyed by operating under incomplete
information relative to a hypothetical full-foresight oracle. This gap
is the primary empirical quantity reported in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. The intractability of computing the
exact solution to \eqref{eq:L2} motivates the Level~3 reformulation:
rather than enumerating the scenario tree, the problem is recast as a
sequential interaction between a policy and an environment, solved
through sampled trajectory experience rather than exhaustive
enumeration.
% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Level~3: Constrained MDP and RL Agent}
\label{subsec:level3}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

The intractability established in Section~\ref{subsec:level2} rules out
exact solution of \eqref{eq:L2} at realistic portfolio scales. The
Level~3 reformulation replaces exhaustive scenario-tree enumeration
with a sequential interaction framework: the portfolio manager is
modelled as a \emph{policy} that observes the current state, commits
an allocation, and receives a reward signal, with the environment
transitioning stochastically to the next state. Optimisation proceeds
through repeated sampled interaction rather than explicit enumeration,
making the computational cost per training step independent of
$|\mathcal{S}| = K^{nH}$.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Constrained Markov Decision Process Formulation}
\label{subsubsec:l3_cmdp}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

\begin{definition}[Portfolio Governance CMDP]
\label{def:cmdp}
The portfolio budget allocation problem at Level~3 is formalised as a
finite-horizon Constrained Markov Decision Process
\citep{altman1999constrained}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M} = \bigl(
    \mathcal{S},\;\mathcal{A},\;\mathcal{T},\;R,\;\mathcal{C},\;\gamma,\;H
  \bigr),
  \label{eq:cmdp_tuple}
\end{equation}
with components defined as follows.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{State space $\mathcal{S}$.}
The state at period $t$ is the portfolio state $S_t$ defined in
\eqref{eq:state}:
\begin{equation}
  S_t \in \mathcal{S} \subseteq
  [0,1]^n \times \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0} \times
  \mathbb{R}^{4n} \times \{0,1,2\}^n \times
  \mathbb{Z}^n_{\geq 0} \times \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
  \label{eq:l3_state}
\end{equation}
collecting, for each project $i$: actual progress
$P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \in [0,1]$, cumulative actual cost
$\mathrm{ACWP}_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$, performance indices
$\mathrm{SPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{CPI}_{i,t}$, $\mathrm{EAC}_{i,t}$,
schedule deviation $\Delta f_{i,t}$, project status
$\mathrm{status}_{i,t} \in \{0,1,2\}$ encoding
$\{\texttt{active}, \texttt{completed}, \texttt{terminated}\}$, and
cure-period counter $\tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$;
together with the portfolio cash balance $B_t \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$.
The state dimension is $8n + 1$ per period. All components are observable.
Two primitives are unobservable at decision time: $\eta_{i,t}$, realised
only after allocation $x_{i,t}$ is committed, and $\lambda_{i,j}$, realised
only after milestone $j$ is certified. Neither is part of the state vector;
both are environment-internal draws that the policy must act without
knowing in advance.

\paragraph{Action space $\mathcal{A}$ and feasible set.}
The raw action space is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A} = \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0},
  \label{eq:l3_action}
\end{equation}
with the state-dependent feasible action set:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}(S_t) = \left\{
    \mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^n_{\geq 0} \;\middle|\;
    \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t} \leq B_t,\quad
    x_{i,t} = 0 \;\text{if}\; \mathrm{status}_{i,t} \neq \texttt{active}
  \right\},
  \label{eq:l3_feasible}
\end{equation}
encoding constraints \eqref{eq:budget} and \eqref{eq:inactive}
directly. The hard liquidity constraint $B_t \geq 0$ is enforced by
construction through the projection operator defined below.

\paragraph{Projection operator.}
To guarantee $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathcal{A}(S_t)$ for any raw
network output $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the
environment applies a two-step projection before executing the
transition. First, allocations to inactive projects are zeroed:
$\tilde{x}_{i,t} \leftarrow 0$ for all $i$ with
$\mathrm{status}_{i,t} \neq \texttt{active}$. Second, the remaining
vector is projected onto the budget simplex:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_t = B_t \cdot
    \frac{\max(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\mathrm{active}},\, \mathbf{0})}
         {\left\|\max(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\mathrm{active}},\,
           \mathbf{0})\right\|_1 \vee 1},
  \label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$ denotes the
sub-vector restricted to active projects, and $\vee$ denotes the
maximum operator ensuring the denominator is at least one when the
agent outputs all-zero or all-negative values. This projection is
differentiable almost everywhere and preserves gradient flow through
the policy network during training \citep{Schulman2017}.

\paragraph{Transition kernel $\mathcal{T}$.}
The stochastic transition
$\mathcal{T}(S_{t+1} \mid S_t, \mathbf{x}_t)$ is defined by the
composition of environment mechanics applied sequentially within each
period:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Draw $\eta_{i,t} \sim F_i$ independently for each active
    project $i$, where $F_i$ is the project-specific efficiency
    distribution calibrated from \citet{Acebes2015, Batselier2017}.
  \item Update progress and actual cost via \eqref{eq:progress_rec}.
  \item Compute EVM signals via \eqref{eq:SPI}--\eqref{eq:EAC}.
  \item Evaluate termination conditions
    \eqref{eq:term_cond1}--\eqref{eq:term_cond2} and update counter
    via \eqref{eq:counter}.
  \item Certify milestones for which $P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}} \geq
    \theta_{i,j}$ \emph{and} $t \geq e_{i,j}$. For each newly certified
    milestone $(i,j)$, draw the payment delay independently:
    $\lambda_{i,j} \sim G_i$ where $G_i$ is the project-specific delay
    distribution over $\{0,1,\ldots,d_i^{\max}\}$ calibrated from
    \citet{Aibinu2006, Assaf1995}; record that $R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}$
    will enter $V_{t + \lambda_{i,j}}$. Compute $V_t$ via
    \eqref{eq:Rnet}--\eqref{eq:Vt} as the sum of all net payments
    whose scheduled receipt period equals $t$.
  \item Update portfolio cash balance via \eqref{eq:balance}.
  \item Execute termination for any project with
    $\tau_{i,t}^{\mathrm{rem}} = 0$, compute
    $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ via \eqref{eq:term_settlement}.
\end{enumerate}
The transition kernel is Markovian: $S_{t+1}$ depends on
$(S_t, \mathbf{x}_t, \boldsymbol{\eta}_t)$ only, not on
$\mathcal{H}_{t-1}$. This is the key structural property that makes
the CMDP formulation exact rather than approximate: the full portfolio
state $S_t$ defined in \eqref{eq:state} is a sufficient statistic for
the history $\mathcal{H}_t$ defined in \eqref{eq:history}.

\paragraph{Reward signal $R$.}
The per-period reward is the discounted net cash flow term from
\eqref{eq:objective}:
\begin{equation}
  r_t(S_t, \mathbf{x}_t, S_{t+1})
    = \gamma^{t-1}\!\left(
        V_t - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}} x_{i,t}
      \right),
  \label{eq:l3_reward}
\end{equation}
where $V_t$ is realised after the transition. The reward contains no
deviation penalty term; completion incentives are embedded structurally
through $R_i^{\mathrm{ret}}$ and $R_{i,M_i}^{\mathrm{net}}$ at
project completion and through $R_i^{\mathrm{term}}$ (possibly
negative) at termination, as established in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:objective}.

\paragraph{Constraint set $\mathcal{C}$.}
The hard liquidity constraint $B_t \geq 0$ is the sole constraint
promoted to the CMDP constraint set:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C} = \bigl\{
    \mathbf{x}_t \in \mathcal{A} \;\mid\; B_{t+1} \geq 0
  \bigr\}.
  \label{eq:l3_constraint}
\end{equation}
This constraint is enforced by the projection \eqref{eq:projection}
rather than through a Lagrangian penalty, ensuring hard satisfaction
at every step rather than satisfaction only in expectation
\citep{altman1999constrained}.

\paragraph{CMDP objective.}
The policy optimisation problem is:
\begin{equation}
  \pi^* = \argmax_{\pi \in \Pi}
    \;J(\pi) \;=\;
    \argmax_{\pi \in \Pi}
    \;\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[
      \sum_{t=1}^{H} r_t(S_t, \mathbf{x}_t, S_{t+1})
    \right],
  \label{eq:l3_objective}
\end{equation}
subject to $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathcal{C}$ for all $t$, where
$\Pi$ denotes the set of all measurable policies satisfying
\eqref{eq:nonanticipativity} and $\mathbb{E}_\pi$ denotes expectation
under the distribution over trajectories induced by $\pi$ and
$\mathcal{T}$. The objective $J(\pi)$ in \eqref{eq:l3_objective} is
identical to the intractable stochastic program \eqref{eq:L2_obj};
the CMDP reformulation does not change the objective but changes the
solution method from scenario-tree enumeration to policy-gradient
optimisation.

\begin{remark}[Relationship to Level~1 upper bound]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:upper_bound}, $J(\pi^*) \leq Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}$
for any causal policy $\pi^*$. The CMDP does not close this gap; it
provides the tractable path to finding the best achievable $J(\pi)$
within $\Pi^{\mathrm{causal}}$. The empirical gap
$Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}} - J(\hat{\pi})$, where $\hat{\pi}$ is the trained
PPO agent, quantifies the combined cost of uncertainty and
approximation error in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
\end{remark}

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Gymnasium-Compatible Environment}
\label{subsubsec:l3_env}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The CMDP $\mathcal{M}$ is implemented as a configurable
Gymnasium-compatible environment \citep{Towers2024}. The environment
exposes the standard \texttt{reset()} / \texttt{step()} interface,
with \texttt{observation\_space} $= \mathcal{S}$ and
\texttt{action\_space} $= \mathcal{A}$. All project and portfolio
parameters (Table~\ref{tab:params}) are passed as a configuration
dictionary at initialisation; the planning horizon $H$ is derived
automatically as $\max_i f_i - \min_i s_i + 1$ from the committed
project set, making the environment fully parameterisable across
contract types and portfolio sizes without code modification.

The observation vector returned at each step is a flattened
concatenation of $S_t$ as defined in \eqref{eq:l3_state}, normalised
to $[0,1]$ per component to facilitate stable policy gradient
estimation. The environment enforces the projection \eqref{eq:projection}
on the raw action vector before applying the transition kernel,
guaranteeing $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathcal{A}(S_t)$ regardless of the
policy output. Episodes terminate when $t = H$ or when all projects
have reached \texttt{completed} or \texttt{terminated} status,
whichever occurs first.

The stochastic efficiency $\eta_{i,t}$ is drawn each period from a
project-specific Beta distribution calibrated from empirical EVM
datasets \citep{Acebes2015, Batselier2017}; the Beta family
accommodates the right-skewed, bounded support structure of empirical
CPI and SPI distributions documented in \citet{Lipke2003} and
\citet{Batselier2017}. Milestone payment timing delays are drawn from
project-specific discrete distributions over $\{0, 1, \ldots,
d_i^{\max}\}$ periods \citep{Aibinu2006}, applied to the net payment
amount $R_{i,j}^{\mathrm{net}}$ at certification without affecting
the certified amount itself.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{PPO Agent and Training Protocol}
\label{subsubsec:l3_ppo}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

\paragraph{Algorithm selection.}
Proximal Policy Optimisation \citep{Schulman2017} is selected as the
solution algorithm for three reasons. First, its clipped surrogate
objective provides stable updates in continuous action spaces without
the step-size sensitivity of vanilla policy gradient methods. Second,
its on-policy sample collection is compatible with the non-stationary
episode structure of the environment, in which the active project set
$\mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}$ changes endogenously across
periods. Third, PPO has demonstrated consistent performance in
operations management domains with similar state-action structure
\citep{Giannoccaro2002, Peng2021}.

\paragraph{PPO surrogate objective.}
At each training iteration, PPO collects a batch of trajectories
$\mathcal{D} = \{\tau_k\}_{k=1}^{N}$ under the current policy
$\pi_\theta$ and optimises the clipped surrogate:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta)
    = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\!\left[
        \min\!\left(
          \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}
               {\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}
          \hat{A}_t,\;\;
          \mathrm{clip}\!\left(
            \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}
                 {\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)},\,
            1-\epsilon,\, 1+\epsilon
          \right)\hat{A}_t
        \right)
      \right],
  \label{eq:ppo_clip}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{A}_t$ is the Generalised Advantage Estimate
\citep{Schulman2016} computed from the value function baseline
$V_\phi(S_t)$ trained jointly with the policy, and $\epsilon$ is the
clipping threshold. The value function shares the lower network layers
with the policy (actor-critic architecture), with separate output
heads for $\pi_\theta$ and $V_\phi$.

\paragraph{Policy architecture.}
The policy network maps the observation vector $S_t \in \mathbb{R}^{8n+1}$
to a raw allocation vector $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^n$
through a fully connected network with layer normalisation. The raw
output is passed through the projection \eqref{eq:projection} before
being applied to the environment, ensuring constraint satisfaction
without a penalty term in \eqref{eq:ppo_clip}. The output
distribution is a diagonal Gaussian with state-dependent mean
$\mu_\theta(S_t)$ and learned log-standard-deviation
$\log\sigma_\theta$, consistent with standard continuous-action PPO
implementations.

\paragraph{Domain randomisation.}
To ensure the learned policy $\hat{\pi}$ generalises across project
types, contract structures, and market conditions rather than
overfitting to a single parameterisation, all parameters in
Table~\ref{tab:params} are randomised within calibrated ranges at the
start of each training episode \citep{Tobin2017}. Formally, at each
episode reset, a parameter vector
$\boldsymbol{\xi} \sim \mathcal{U}(\boldsymbol{\Xi})$ is sampled from
a calibrated uniform distribution over the parameter space
$\boldsymbol{\Xi}$, and the environment is initialised with
$\boldsymbol{\xi}$. The trained policy $\hat{\pi}$ implicitly
conditions on the observable consequences of $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ (as
reflected in $S_t$) rather than on $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ directly,
producing a policy that is robust to parameter uncertainty without
requiring an explicit model of the parameter distribution at
deployment \citep{Tobin2017}.

\paragraph{Validation protocol.}
Walk-forward validation \citep{Cerqueira2020} is used as the empirical
evaluation protocol to guard against data leakage given the
time-series structure of project cash-flow data. The training set
consists of episodes whose parameter draws fall within the calibrated
historical range; the test set consists of held-out parameter
configurations drawn from the tails of $\boldsymbol{\Xi}$,
representing out-of-distribution contract and market conditions. All
performance metrics in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} are reported on
the held-out test set.

% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
\subsubsection{Evaluation Baselines}
\label{subsubsec:l3_baselines}
% - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

The trained agent $\hat{\pi}$ is evaluated against three baselines
that span the tractability-optimality spectrum established by the
three-level arc.

\paragraph{Baseline 1 — Level~1 MILP oracle.}
The MILP \eqref{eq:L1} is solved on each test episode's realised
efficiency trajectory $(\eta_{i,t}(\omega))_{i,t}$ using a
commercial solver. Its objective value $Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}}(\omega)$
is the per-episode upper bound from Proposition~\ref{prop:upper_bound}.
The gap $Z^*_{\mathrm{L1}} - J(\hat{\pi})$ is the primary performance
metric.

\paragraph{Baseline 2 — Two-stage stochastic program.}
A two-stage approximation of \eqref{eq:L2} is solved by fixing
first-stage decisions $\mathbf{x}_1$ and optimising over the
expected recourse across $N_s$ sampled scenarios for subsequent
stages \citep{Birge2011}. This represents the best tractable exact
method available within the stochastic programming tradition and
quantifies the value of the full sequential policy over a rolling
two-stage approximation.

\paragraph{Baseline 3 — Proportional heuristic.}
At each period $t$, the available balance $B_t$ is distributed across
active projects proportionally to their remaining planned expenditure
$\mathrm{BAC}_i(1 - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}})$:
\begin{equation}
  x_{i,t}^{\mathrm{prop}}
    = B_t \cdot
      \frac{\mathrm{BAC}_i(1 - P_{i,t}^{\mathrm{actual}})}
           {\displaystyle\sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_t^{\mathrm{active}}}
            \mathrm{BAC}_j(1 - P_{j,t}^{\mathrm{actual}})}.
  \label{eq:heuristic}
\end{equation}
This rule is parameter-free and represents current practice in
portfolio management information systems: budget is distributed
in proportion to remaining work without regard to contract
profitability, schedule urgency, or termination risk. The gap between
the PPO agent and this baseline quantifies the practical value of the
learned policy over the incumbent approach.
