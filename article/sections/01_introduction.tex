\subsection{Motivation and Context}

Project portfolio management (PPM) is a fundamental organizational capability for maintaining competitive advantage and achieving strategic objectives. In project-based industries, portfolio decisions determine which initiatives are selected, prioritized, and funded under limited resources, conflicting criteria, and uncertain execution outcomes \citep{archer1999integrated,cooper2001portfolio,martinsuo2013project,killen2008project}. These decisions become particularly critical in large-scale engineering environments, where organizations must coordinate multiple concurrent projects while balancing strategic value, financial feasibility, schedule pressure, and risk exposure.

In the Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) sector, especially in oil and gas projects, portfolio budgeting is complicated by heterogeneous contract structures, long project durations, capital intensity, and uncertain cash-flow behavior \citep{merrow2011industrial,cui2010systems,kenley1986construction,kaka1993towards}. Budget allocation must account for both cash outflows associated with procurement, labor, equipment, and subcontractors, and delayed cash inflows tied to contractual milestones and client approvals. Consequently, portfolio budgeting is not merely a one-time allocation problem; it is a sequential decision process in which current funding choices influence future project progress, liquidity conditions, and portfolio-level performance.

This challenge is amplified by persistent uncertainty in project execution. Large industrial and infrastructure projects frequently experience delays and cost overruns, and such deviations can propagate through both schedules and financial plans \citep{flyvbjerg2002underestimating,flyvbjerg2003underestimating,love2013probability,love2016relationship}. Earned value indicators such as the Schedule Performance Index (SPI) and Cost Performance Index (CPI) provide a compact representation of execution performance and can support project monitoring and control \citep{fleming2016earned,lipke2009prediction,vanhoucke2012integrated}. However, these indicators also underscore the difficulty of making reliable budget allocation decisions when future project states, payment realizations, and performance deviations remain uncertain. Therefore, portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows requires methods that can reason across multiple projects, multiple time periods, and stochastic state transitions.

The broader literature on project portfolio management similarly characterizes such environments by large data volumes, multiple decision criteria, resource constraints, and the need for investment optimization. These characteristics are consistent with prior research on project portfolio selection, project scheduling under uncertainty, and construction cash-flow forecasting \citep{gutjahr2013project,doerner2006pareto,khanzadi2018integrating,golenko1997stochastic,ballesteros2015resource,barraza2007probabilistic}. Taken together, they motivate the development of intelligent decision-support methods when static or manually driven budgeting approaches become insufficient.

\subsection{Research Gap}

A substantial body of literature has addressed project portfolio selection, prioritization, and scheduling through operations research, multi-criteria decision-making, stochastic programming, and metaheuristic optimization \citep{archer1999integrated,liesio2007portfolio,klerides2019stochastic,elshaer2014genetic}. These methods provide valuable structure for complex decision problems, but they are often less suitable when portfolio budget allocation must respond to evolving project conditions under uncertainty. In many existing formulations, project cash flows, budget requirements, and performance trajectories are treated as fixed inputs, deterministic plans, or exogenous scenarios. This limits their ability to capture the interaction between current allocation decisions and future project and financial states.

A central limitation in this context is that portfolio budgeting in EPC environments is inherently sequential. Budget releases made at one time step affect project execution, milestone realization, cash availability, and subsequent allocation opportunities. Yet much of the existing literature approaches project portfolio decisions as static optimization, stage-wise planning, or scheduling problems rather than as a learned sequential policy problem under uncertainty. As a result, the adaptive linkage between observed portfolio state and subsequent allocation action remains insufficiently modeled.

Recent advances in machine learning and reinforcement learning provide a promising direction for this class of problems. Reinforcement learning is specifically designed for sequential decision-making under uncertainty and has demonstrated value in operations, scheduling, and process control settings \citep{puterman1994markov,sutton2018reinforcement,wauters2016learning,chen2018reinforcement,hubbs2020deep,bengio2021machine}. Nevertheless, its application to project portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows remains limited. There is still a need for a reproducible framework that can integrate project-level uncertainty, cash-flow timing, milestone-based payments, and adaptive resource allocation within a portfolio-level sequential decision architecture.

A further practical constraint is data availability. Detailed industrial project portfolio data are typically confidential and difficult to release publicly, which creates a barrier to reproducible experimentation. As a result, the literature lacks sufficiently transparent benchmark environments for testing adaptive portfolio budgeting policies. This gap motivates the use of literature-calibrated synthetic data as a practical foundation for developing and evaluating learning-based decision-support systems \citep{jordon2022synthetic,xu2019modeling}.

\subsection{Proposed Approach and Contributions}

To address these gaps, this paper develops a deep reinforcement learning framework for optimal project portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows. The problem is modeled as a sequential decision process in which an agent observes the evolving state of a project portfolio and allocates constrained financial resources over time. The state representation incorporates project-level and portfolio-level information, including budget at completion, planned duration, contractual cash-flow patterns, performance indicators, milestone-based payment structures, and liquidity-related variables.

The framework is designed to learn an allocation policy directly from repeated interaction with a stochastic simulation environment. Rather than relying on predefined deterministic decision rules or repeated re-optimization procedures, the agent learns how present allocation choices affect future portfolio states and cumulative performance over time. To learn this policy, the study employs deep reinforcement learning, with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) as the primary training algorithm \citep{schulman2017proximal}, consistent with the broader reinforcement-learning literature on stochastic control and policy optimization \citep{sutton2018reinforcement,haarnoja2018soft}.

After training, the learned policy can also be deployed within the simulation environment to generate a forward policy rollout over a specified horizon, such as the next 12 time steps, in order to illustrate plausible portfolio evolution and corresponding recommended allocations. This forward policy rollout should be interpreted as a simulated policy-driven trajectory rather than as a realized future observation. Its purpose is to demonstrate the behavioral implications of the learned allocation policy under the modeled environment dynamics until actual future project states become available.

Because real EPC portfolio datasets are typically inaccessible due to confidentiality restrictions, the framework uses a literature-calibrated synthetic data generation process. This allows reproducible experimentation while preserving consistency with known characteristics of project cash flows, schedule variability, and portfolio structure. The synthetic environment is intended as a research testbed that can later be refined and calibrated using anonymized organizational data.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A sequential formulation of project portfolio budgeting:} The study formulates EPC portfolio budgeting under uncertain cash flows as a dynamic decision problem in which allocations, project progress, and liquidity conditions evolve jointly over time.

    \item \textbf{A custom reinforcement learning environment for portfolio budgeting:} The paper develops an environment that captures project-level uncertainty, cash inflows and outflows, milestone-based payments, and portfolio-level financial constraints.

    \item \textbf{A deep reinforcement learning policy for adaptive allocation:} The framework trains an agent to learn budget allocation decisions directly from stochastic interaction with the environment.

    \item \textbf{A forward policy rollout capability for trajectory demonstration:} The study shows how the trained policy can be used to simulate plausible future portfolio trajectories and recommended allocations over a finite horizon.

    \item \textbf{A literature-calibrated synthetic data framework:} The study provides a reproducible synthetic portfolio-generation procedure to address the industrial data confidentiality barrier.
\end{enumerate}
