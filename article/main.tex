\begin{abstract}

    Large contracted project portfolios expose governing boards to a recurring
    operational decision absent from the formal literature: how to allocate budget
    across active projects at each discrete period to maximise discounted net cash
    flow under cash-flow uncertainty, FIDIC contract mechanics, and the options to
    intervene in or terminate underperforming projects. This paper addresses that
    gap through a three-level modelling arc. \textbf{Level~1} formulates the
    problem as a deterministic full-foresight Mixed-Integer Linear Program whose
    optimum constitutes a provable upper bound on any causal policy operating under
    uncertainty. \textbf{Level~2} reintroduces stochastic contractor performance,
    establishing that the resulting multi-stage stochastic program is computationally
    intractable at realistic portfolio scales due to exponential scenario-tree
    growth. \textbf{Level~3} reformulates the problem as a Constrained Markov
    Decision Process, implemented as a configurable Gymnasium-compatible environment
    calibrated from anonymised EPC contractor data, on which a Proximal Policy
    Optimisation agent is trained and evaluated against the Level~1 oracle, a
    two-stage stochastic program, and a rule-based heuristic. The environment
    itself---encoding advance payment recovery, milestone-based payments, retention
    release, symmetric deviation penalties, and a two-condition termination
    gate---constitutes the primary contribution.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} project portfolio management; budget allocation;
reinforcement learning; constrained Markov decision process; earned value
management; FIDIC contracts; stochastic optimisation; mixed-integer linear
programming.
\end{abstract}
