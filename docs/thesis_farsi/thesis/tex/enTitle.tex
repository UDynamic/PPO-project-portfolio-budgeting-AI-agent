\enabstract{
  Allocating a shared capital reserve across a portfolio of simultaneously
  executing contracted projects is a high-frequency, high-stakes decision
  for engineering contractors. Classical approaches either assume full
  foresight or reduce the portfolio to a single project, leaving no
  principled tool for the sequential, multi-project setting under
  uncertainty. This paper addresses that gap with a verified,
  Gymnasium-compatible simulation environment designed as the learning
  substrate for reinforcement learning agents targeting portfolio-level
  investment decisions.

  We formalise the problem as a Markov Decision Process in which a
  portfolio manager allocates budget fractions across $N$ concurrent
  projects at each discrete period. The state encodes Earned Value
  Management indicators, S-curve planned progress, milestone payment
  lookaheads, and a per-project breach-tolerance counter. Transition
  dynamics reproduce the full FIDIC contract cash-flow cycle---advance
  payment and recovery, milestone gross payments with retention and
  interest, and a two-condition termination gate---with the reward signal
  being the $\gamma$-discounted portfolio net cash flow, accumulating to
  an episode NPV. A full-foresight oracle bound is derived analytically
  and verified by exhaustive enumeration over the Cartesian product of
  per-period action sets, confirming no alternative allocation sequence
  yields a higher cumulative discounted return.

  The environment ships with a SQLite audit ledger that records every
  allocation, cash-flow event, and breach flag, eliminating black-box
  opacity. Deterministic greedy heuristics and the verified
  full-information optimum are evaluated across benchmark configurations
  of increasing complexity. The optimality gap widens by more than an
  order of magnitude from the simplest to the most complex configuration;
  on the hardest instance every rule-based policy yields a loss while the
  oracle remains profitable---confirming that shared-budget coordination
  cannot be recovered by any fixed allocation rule and establishing a
  well-defined learning target for future RL agents.
}
