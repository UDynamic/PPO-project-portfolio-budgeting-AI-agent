\latinkeywords{Project Portfolio Management, Budget Allocation, Markov Decision Process, Reinforcement Learning, FIDIC Contracts, Earned Value Management, Gymnasium Environment}
\en-abstract{
    Allocating a shared capital reserve across a portfolio of
  simultaneously executing contracted projects is a high-frequency,
  high-stakes decision for engineering contractors. Classical
  approaches either assume full foresight or collapse the portfolio
  to a single project, leaving practitioners with no principled tool
  for the sequential, multi-project setting under real-world
  uncertainty. This paper addresses that gap by delivering a
  verified, Gymnasium-compatible simulation environment designed as
  the learning substrate for reinforcement learning agents targeting
  portfolio-level investment decisions.
  
  We formalise the problem as a Markov Decision Process in which a
  portfolio manager allocates budget fractions across $N$ concurrent
  projects at each discrete period. The state encodes Earned Value
  Management indicators, S-curve planned progress, milestone payment
  lookaheads, and a per-project breach-tolerance counter. The
  transition dynamics reproduce the full FIDIC contract cash-flow
  cycle: advance payment and recovery, milestone gross payments with
  retention and interest, and a two-condition termination gate. The
  reward signal is the $\gamma$-discounted portfolio net cash flow,
  accumulating to an episode NPV. A full-foresight oracle bound is
  derived analytically, establishing the ceiling against which
  learned agents will be evaluated.
  
  The environment ships with a SQLite audit ledger that records every
  allocation, cash-flow event, and breach flag, eliminating the
  black-box opacity inherent to learned policies. To verify
  environment mechanics and quantify the performance ceiling available
  to future agents, deterministic greedy heuristics and a manually
  computed full-information optimum are evaluated across benchmark
  configurations of increasing structural complexity. The optimality
  gap widens by more than an order of magnitude from the simplest to
  the most complex configuration, and on the hardest instance every
  rule-based policy yields a loss while the full-information optimum
  remains profitable --- confirming that shared-budget coordination
  cannot be recovered by any fixed allocation rule and establishing a
  well-defined learning target for the RL agent.
}
