\latinkeywords{Project Portfolio Management, Budget Allocation, Markov Decision Process, Reinforcement Learning, FIDIC Contracts, Earned Value Management, Gymnasium Environment}
\en-abstract{
    Allocating a shared capital reserve across a portfolio of simultaneously
    executing contracted projects is a high-frequency, high-stakes decision for
    engineering contractors. Classical approaches either assume full foresight or
    collapse the portfolio to a single project, leaving practitioners with no
    principled tool for the sequential, multi-project setting under real-world
    uncertainty. This paper closes that gap by delivering a complete, verified
    simulation environment that makes the problem tractable for data-driven methods.
  
    We formalise the problem as a Markov Decision Process in which a
    portfolio manager allocates budget fractions across $N$ concurrent projects at
    each discrete period. The state encodes Earned Value Management indicators,
    S-curve planned progress, milestone payment lookaheads, and a per-project
    breach-tolerance counter. The transition dynamics reproduce the full FIDIC
    contract cash-flow cycle: advance payment and recovery, progress-weighted
    milestone gross payments, retention withholding and release, interest charges
    on treasury draws, and a two-condition termination gate. The reward signal is
    the $\gamma$-discounted portfolio net cash flow, accumulating to an episode NPV
    that serves as the primary performance criterion. A full-foresight oracle
    bound is derived analytically, and the exact stochastic optimisation problem
    is shown to be intractable at portfolio scale via scenario-tree enumeration,
    formally motivating the MDP/RL formulation over classical stochastic
    programming.
  
    The environment is released as a configurable, Gymnasium-compatible package
    ---verified across 51 checks in 10 test sections--- with a SQLite audit ledger
    that records every allocation, cash-flow event, and breach flag, eliminating
    the black-box problem inherent to learned policies. To establish a performance
    baseline and motivate the need for learned agents, we run three deterministic
    greedy heuristics (Reach-Plan, Catchup-T, and Catchup-Next-T) and a manually
    computed full-information optimum across four benchmark configurations of
    increasing portfolio complexity. The best-heuristic optimality gap widens
    systematically with complexity, from 3.40 NPV units on an isolated
    single-project instance to 72.15 units on a three-project portfolio with
    overlapping cash flows --- a 21-fold increase --- and on the most complex
    configuration every greedy heuristic yields a loss while the full-information
    optimum remains profitable. These results confirm that standard rule-based
    allocation is structurally insufficient under shared-budget coordination and
    establish a quantified, well-defined improvement target for reinforcement
    learning agents. Training such agents (e.g. via PPO), evaluating them against
    a formal Linear Programming or Dynamic Programming bound, and exploring
    LLM-augmented allocation policies are identified as the natural next steps,
    addressed in subsequent work.
}
