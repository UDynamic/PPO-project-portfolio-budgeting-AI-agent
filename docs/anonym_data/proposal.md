## The Core Reality First

Contractors are **extremely** protective of project data. Your approach needs to answer their unspoken question immediately:

> *"What's in it for us and what's the risk?"*

Your pitch must minimize perceived risk and maximize perceived benefit before they even think about saying no.

---

## Who You're Actually Talking To

You need to identify the right person inside each company. Going to the wrong person kills the request before it starts.

**Right people:**
- PMO Director / Portfolio Manager
- Chief Operating Officer
- Head of Project Controls
- Business Development Director

**Wrong people:**
- Project managers (no authority)
- Finance department (will flag compliance risk)
- HR (irrelevant)

---

## The 3-Step Approach Strategy

### Step 1 — Warm Introduction, Not Cold Email

Never cold email a data request. It will be ignored or rejected immediately.

The right sequence:

```
Personal connection → LinkedIn message → 
formal email → meeting → data agreement
```

If you have any existing connection to these companies through your university, your supervisor, or your financial aid company — use it. A single warm introduction is worth 10 cold emails.

**Action:** Ask your thesis supervisor explicitly — *"Do you have any contacts at contractor firms who might support thesis research?"* Supervisors often have industry connections they don't volunteer unless asked.

---

### Step 2 — The Initial Outreach Message

Keep it short. Do not explain the whole thesis. One paragraph, one ask.---

### Step 3 — The Confirmation Letter

Once they verbally agree, you need a formal letter to make it official and protect both sides. This is what journals and thesis committees want to see as evidence of data legitimacy.---

## What Data To Actually Ask For

Be specific when they agree. Vague requests cause delays. Ask for exactly this:

```
Per project (no project names needed):

- Project type (construction / IT / infrastructure)
- Planned duration (months)
- BAC (as index, e.g. normalized to 1.0)
- Monthly CPI values across project life
- Monthly SPI values across project life
- Final cost overrun ratio (Actual/BAC)
- Final schedule overrun ratio
- Number of milestones
- Whether project was completed, terminated, or ongoing
```

This is the minimum you need to calibrate your efficiency parameter distributions and S-curve shape parameters. Nothing sensitive, nothing identifiable.

## The Full Sequence Summary

```
1. Identify right person (PMO Director / COO)
        ↓
2. Warm intro through supervisor or network
        ↓
3. Send initial outreach email (formal version)
        ↓
4. 20-minute call — explain research, show one slide
        ↓
5. Send confirmation letter template for them to sign
        ↓
6. Receive signed letter + data
        ↓
7. Acknowledge in thesis as "anonymous research partner"
```
